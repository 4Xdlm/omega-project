Chapter 12
Bolton

The scent hits me first. Rogue musk mixed with something sharper, more desperate. Wrong. Everything about it is wrong.


I freeze on the patrol path, every instinct screaming danger. The forest has gone too quiet, the kind of silence that comes right before violence. My wolf surges beneath my skin, hackles raised, ready for a fight.


"Dax," I whisper into my radio. "We've got company. Western border, half a mile from the creek."


Static crackles back. "How many?"


Before I can answer, they burst from the undergrowth.


Three rogues, lean and scarred, their eyes wild with hunger and desperation. The largest one, a grizzled male with matted brown fur and yellowed fangs, launches himself at me with a snarl that echoes through the trees.


Training kicks in. I shift mid-dodge, bones cracking and reforming in seconds, my human form giving way to the dark wolf that's been pacing beneath my skin. The rogue's claws whistle past my ear as I roll, coming up in a crouch with my teeth bared.


The second rogue, smaller but faster, circles to my left while the third hangs back, watching for an opening. Classic pack hunting formation, which means these aren't mindless ferals. They're organized. Dangerous.


The big one charges again, and this time I meet him head-on. We collide in a tangle of fur and fury, rolling across the forest floor in a deadly dance. His teeth snap inches from my throat, but I twist away, my own jaws clamping down on his shoulder.


He howls, the sound raw and enraged, and throws me off with a surge of desperate strength. I hit a tree trunk hard enough to rattle my bones, but I'm back on my feet before he can press the advantage.


The smaller rogue sees his chance and darts in from the side. His claws rake across my ribs, drawing blood, sending fire through my nervous system. Pain sharpens my focus, strips away everything except the immediate need to survive.


I catch him with a backhand that sends him sprawling, then whip around to face the leader again. He's bleeding from the shoulder wound, but his eyes burn with the kind of fanatical determination that makes rogues so dangerous. They have nothing left to lose.


"You cannot protect her forever," he snarls, his voice distorted by his partial shift. "The weak Luna will fall."


Ice floods my veins. Maya. This isn't random. This is targeted.


The rage that explodes through me is white-hot and completely beyond reason. My wolf roars to the surface, bringing with him every protective instinct, every territorial claim, every ounce of Alpha authority in my bloodline.


I don't shift back to human form. I embrace the wolf completely.


What happens next isn't a fight. It's a slaughter.


I move like liquid violence, faster than thought, driven by pure instinct. The smaller rogue tries to run, but I'm on him before he makes it three steps. My jaws close around his throat, and his struggles cease with a wet gurgle.


The third rogue, who'd been hanging back, finally joins the fight, but he's too late and too slow. I meet his charge with claws extended, ripping through fur and flesh like paper. He drops without a sound.


The leader, wounded and alone, backs against a tree with his lips pulled back in a desperate snarl. "You think you've won? There are more of us. Many more. And we know her weaknesses."


"You know nothing," I growl, stalking closer. Every step is deliberate, predatory. "And you'll never get the chance to learn."


He lunges in a final, desperate attack. I sidestep easily and catch him mid-leap, my claws finding the soft spot beneath his ribs. His eyes go wide with shock, then empty.


The forest falls silent again, but this time it's the natural quiet of a threat eliminated. I stand among the bodies, chest heaving. The rage is still burning in my veins, demanding more violence, more protection of what's mine.


It takes long minutes before I can shift back to human form, and even then, my hands are shaking with residual fury. I pull out my radio with fingers that feel clumsy and strange.


"Dax. Threat eliminated. Three rogues down."


"Bolton. Are you hurt?"


I look down at myself. My shirt is torn, blood seeping through the fabric from the claw marks on my ribs. But I'm alive, and that's what matters. "Nothing serious. Send a cleanup crew to my position."


"Copy that. Bolton... how the hell did three rogues get past our patrols?"


The question I've been trying not to think about. Our security is tight, has been since the first rogue sightings weeks ago. For them to get this deep into our territory without detection means either our patrols missed something crucial, or...


Or someone gave them inside information.


Cassie's face flashes through my mind, along with her cryptic comments about problems solving themselves when the right people take action. My jaw clenches so hard it aches.


"I don't know," I tell Dax, but I'm lying. I have a pretty good idea.


By the time the cleanup crew arrives, I've managed to get my breathing under control, but the rage is still simmering just beneath the surface. Three rogues, deep in our territory, talking about Maya's weaknesses. This wasn't a random attack. This was reconnaissance. A probe to test our defenses and gather intelligence.


And they knew exactly where to find me.


"Alpha." One of the senior pack members, approaches with his head slightly lowered in respect. "Are you sure you're all right? That's a lot of blood."


"I'm fine." The words come out sharper than I intend. "Just get the bodies out of here and sweep the area for any signs of how they got through."


He nods and signals to the others. They work efficiently, professionally, but I can see the questions in their eyes. How did this happen? Why weren't we prepared? What if there had been more of them?


What if they'd made it to the pack house? To Maya?


The thought sends another surge of protective fury through my system. I need to check on her, need to see with my own eyes that she's safe. But first, I need to make a decision that's been eating at me since the attack ended.


Do I report this to my father?


The smart move, the proper move, would be to go straight to our Alpha. Tell him about the attack, about my suspicions regarding Cassie, about the rogues' specific mention of Maya. Let him handle the politics and the investigation while I focus on protection and security.


But that would mean admitting I couldn't handle it myself. That despite weeks of increased patrols and heightened vigilance, the rogues still found a way through. It would mean looking weak, uncertain, like a young Alpha who needs his father's guidance to manage a crisis.


The pack is already questioning Maya's fitness as Luna. If they start questioning my ability to protect them, to lead them...


No. I can handle this myself. I have to.


By the time I make it back to the pack house, the adrenaline has faded, leaving me exhausted and aching. The claw marks on my ribs throb with each breath, and there's a persistent headache building behind my eyes. But I'm alive, and our territory is secure. That has to be enough.


I need to find Maya. Need to hold her, breathe in her scent, reassure myself that she's safe and whole. The mate bond has been pulling at me since the attack, a constant low-level anxiety that won't ease until I see her with my own eyes.


I find her in the common room, curled up in one of the oversized armchairs with a book in her lap. She looks small, fragile, nothing like the fierce woman who held a partial shift for ten minutes just days ago. The sight of her sends relief flooding through my system, followed immediately by a surge of protective tenderness.


She's safe. She's here. She's mine to protect.


"Maya." Her name comes out rougher than I intend, heavy with everything I've been carrying.


She looks up, and I see her eyes widen as she takes in my appearance. The torn shirt, the dried blood, the barely controlled tension radiating from every line of my body.


"Bolton? What happened?" She's on her feet in an instant, crossing to me with worry written across her face. "You're hurt."


"It's nothing serious." I catch her hands as she reaches for the tears in my shirt, needing the contact, the confirmation that she's real and safe. "Just some scratches."


"Scratches?" Her voice goes up an octave. "Bolton, there's blood all over you. What aren't you telling me?"


The concern in her voice, it should be comforting. Instead, it just reminds me how close I came to losing her today. How close we all came to disaster.


"Rogues," I say simply. "Three of them. They got past our patrols somehow."


Her face goes white. "Rogues? Here? In our territory?"


"They're dead now." The words come out flat, emotionless, but underneath I can feel the rage stirring again. "They won't be reporting back to whoever sent them."


"Whoever sent them?" Maya's grip on my hands tightens. "Bolton, what do you mean?"


I should tell her. Should explain about Cassie, about my suspicions, about the rogues' specific mention of her weaknesses. But looking at her now, seeing the fear already creeping into her eyes, I can't bring myself to add to her burden.


She's been struggling enough lately. Ever since that morning when something went wrong during training, she's been distant, withdrawn, like she's building walls between us. The last thing she needs is confirmation that there are enemies inside our own pack working against her.


"Nothing," I lie. "Just rogues being rogues. Desperate and stupid."


But Maya's not stupid, and I can see the doubt flickering in her expression. She knows I'm not telling her everything. The mate bond makes it almost impossible to lie to each other completely, carrying emotional undertones that words can't hide.


"Bolton." Her voice is quiet, careful. "You're scaring me. Please just tell me what really happened."


The plea in her voice nearly breaks my resolve. Nearly has me spilling everything—the attack, the suspicions, the growing certainty that someone in our own pack is working against us. But then I remember the way she looked after that failed training session, the defeat in her eyes, the way she's been pulling away from me ever since.


She's not ready for this. Not yet.


"I told you what happened," I say, gently extracting my hands from hers. "Some rogues got through. I dealt with them. End of story."


The distance I put between us is only a few inches, but it might as well be miles. I see the hurt flash across her face, quickly followed by something that looks like resignation.


"Right," she says quietly. "Of course. The big, strong Alpha handles everything himself."


There's an edge to her voice that puts me on guard. "Maya—"


"No, I get it." She takes a step back, wrapping her arms around herself in a gesture that's becoming too familiar. "You don't need to explain anything to the weak Luna who can't even control her own wolf. Why would you trust me with pack business?"


"That's not what this is about."


"Isn't it?" Her eyes flash with hurt and anger. "Because from where I'm standing, it looks like you're shutting me out. Again."


The accusation stings because there's truth in it. I am shutting her out, but not for the reasons she thinks. Not because I don't trust her or because I think she's weak. Because I'm trying to protect her. Because I love her too much to watch her break under the weight of knowing how many people want to destroy her.


But I can't say any of that. Can't admit that my need to protect her is stronger than my respect for her right to know. So instead, I fall back on authority, on the cold distance that's always been my shield.


"This is pack security, Maya. Alpha business. When you're ready to be Luna, we can discuss it."


The words are out before I can stop them, cruel and cutting and completely unfair. I see them hit her like physical blows, see the way she flinches and goes very, very still.


"When I'm ready." Her voice is barely above a whisper. "Right. Well, I guess that says everything, doesn't it?"


"Maya, I didn't mean—"


"Yes, you did." She's backing toward the door now, her face a mask of hurt and humiliation. "You meant exactly what you said. I'm not ready. I'm not strong enough. I'm not Luna material."


"That's not true."


"Then why won't you tell me what's really going on?" The question hangs between us like a blade. "Why won't you trust me?"


Because I'm terrified of losing you. Because the thought of you getting hurt makes me lose control in ways I can't afford. Because I'd rather carry the weight of the world alone than watch it crush you.


But I can't say any of that. Can't admit that my strength is actually weakness, that my protection is actually fear.


So I say nothing.


Maya stares at me for a long moment, and I watch something die in her eyes. Some last vestige of hope or trust or love that I've finally managed to kill with my silence.


"I see," she says quietly. "Well, thank you for making it clear where I stand."


She turns and walks out of the room, her spine straight and her head high, but I can feel her pain through the mate bond like a knife between my ribs. Every step she takes away from me sends another wave of loss and longing through the connection, but I don't follow.


I can't follow. Because if I do, I'll tell her everything, and then I'll lose her anyway. Better to lose her to distance than to death.


The common room feels empty without her presence, cold and lifeless. I sink into the chair she'd been occupying, still warm from her body, and drop my head into my hands.


The rogues are dead, but the threat they represented is very much alive. Somewhere out there, more of them are planning their next move. And somewhere in here, someone is helping them.


I need to find Cassie. Need to confront her, get proof of what she's been doing. But first, I need to tend to these wounds and figure out how to protect Maya without destroying what's left of our relationship.


If there's anything left to save.


The mate bond stretches between us like a fraying rope, carrying her pain and my regret in equal measure. I've hurt her. Again. In my desperate attempt to shield her from danger, I've become the danger myself.


But I don't know how to stop. Don't know how to balance protection with partnership, strength with vulnerability. I only know that I love her more than my own life, and that love is tearing us both apart.


Outside, the sun is setting, painting the windows in shades of red and gold. Beautiful and peaceful, giving no hint of the violence that stained the forest just hours ago.


The storm is coming. I can feel it building, pressure mounting from all sides. And when it breaks, I pray I'll be strong enough to weather it.


Strong enough to save the woman I love, even if it means losing her in the process.

I'm sitting on the back porch steps, staring at my hands like they might hold answers, when Rene finds me.


"Mind if I join you?" she asks, her voice carrying that gentle warmth that always makes me feel like maybe I belong here after all.

Rene settles beside me with a soft sigh, her presence immediately calming. She doesn't ask what's wrong or try to fill the silence with empty reassurances. She just sits with me, steady and patient, like she has all the time in the world.


"You know," she says eventually, "when I first came to this pack, I couldn't shift for three months."


I glance at her in surprise. Rene, who moves with such natural grace, who seems to embody everything a Luna should be, struggled with something as basic as shifting?


"Really?"


"Really." She smiles, but there's a shadow of old pain in her eyes. "I was so terrified of letting the wolf out, of losing control, that I locked her away completely. Grayson was patient with me, but I could see the worry in his eyes. The pack needed a Luna who could protect them, and I couldn't even access half of what I was."


"What changed?"


"I realized I was approaching it all wrong." She turns to face me more fully, her green eyes serious. "I was trying to control my wolf instead of working with her. I was treating her like an enemy instead of a partner."


The words hit closer to home than I'd like to admit. How many times have I thought of my wolf as something to be contained, managed, kept separate from who I really am?


"It's scary," I whisper. "What if I let her out and she's... too much?"


"What if she's exactly what you need to be?"


Before I can answer, the sound of children's laughter drifts from the front yard. Tommy's voice rises above the others, bright and excited.


"Tell us the story again! About Red and the wolves!"


My stomach clenches. Even the children know the nickname now. Even they see me as the helpless girl who stumbled into danger.


But Rosie's voice stops me cold.


"I don't like that story," she announces with the fierce certainty only a six-year-old can muster. "Red doesn't get to win. She just gets saved."


"That's how the story goes," one of the older boys argues.


"Well, it's stupid," Rosie declares. "If I was Red, I'd learn to fight back. I wouldn't just wait for someone to save me."


Rene and I exchange a look, and I see understanding dawn in her expression.


"Smart girl," Rene murmurs.


"She's right, though." The admission comes out cracked and small. "That's all I am. The girl who needs saving. Bolton thinks I'm too weak to protect myself. Too unstable to be trusted with pack business."


"Is that what you think?"


"It's what everyone thinks." I stand abruptly, pacing to the porch railing. "The elders, Cassie, probably half the pack by now. They call me Red, like I'm some fairy tale character who brings nothing but trouble."


"Maya." Rene's voice carries a note of steel I've never heard before. "Look at me."


I turn reluctantly, expecting to see pity or gentle disappointment. Instead, I find fierce determination.


"Do you know why they call you Red?"


"Because I'm helpless. Because I wandered into your territory like some lost little girl."


"No." She stands, moving to join me at the railing. "They call you Red because you're dangerous to them. Because you represent change, growth, the possibility that things don't have to stay the same forever. Red Riding Hood wasn't the victim in that story, Maya. She was the catalyst. She brought the wolf into the light."

"Strength isn't about never falling, Maya. It's about getting back up every time you do."

If I was Red, I'd learn to fight back.

The girl who's been so busy trying to fit into someone else's idea of what she should be that she forgot to figure out who she actually is.

"Worthy of myself," I correct, and realize it's true. "I've been so focused on proving I deserve him that I forgot I need to deserve me first."


"Now you're getting it."

My home, if I'm brave enough to claim it.

Hello, I whisper in the quiet space of my mind. I'm sorry I've been fighting you. I'm ready to listen now.

Someone who's finally ready to claim her place.

"I found my wolf," I tell him simply.


His hand comes up to cup my cheek, his thumb brushing across my skin with reverent gentleness. "You're different."


"I'm me. Finally, completely me." I lean into his touch, feeling the mate bond settle between us like a bridge finally completed. "And we need to talk."

Chapter 19
Maya

The scent of spilt apple juice and faint crayon still clings to Rene’s living room, a comforting echo of this morning. After the chaos, the betrayal, the raw triumph of the woods, this quiet domesticity feels like a precious anchor.

Little Red Riding Hood.

Good changes, he sends back, a quiet certainty in his tone. You found your fire, Maya.

This is the beginning. Not just of my reign as Luna, but of a new narrative. A story where the wolf isn't the monster, but the hero. And I am ready to write every single chapter.

Chapter 20
Bolton

The air in the Alpha’s meeting hall is thick with tension, a heavy silence punctuated only by the scuff of boots and the low, uneasy murmurs of the pack elders. They’re gathered around the long, polished oak table, their faces etched with grim concern. Dax stands at my side, a solid, reassuring presence, his gaze unwavering on my father at the head of the table. Kade, the rogue I brought down last night, sits slumped in a chair by the door, guarded by two of our strongest warriors. He’s bruised and defeated, but his eyes still hold a flicker of defiance. The other captured rogues are secured in the holding cells beneath the pack house.


My own ribs throb with a dull ache, a constant reminder of the fight. But the physical pain is nothing compared to the turmoil in my gut. This isn’t just about the rogues anymore. It’s about Cassie. And it’s about Maya.


She sits across from me, her posture straight, her eyes resolute. She has changed into fresh clothes, but the faint smudges of dirt on her cheek and the lingering wildness in her hair tell their own story. She’s my Luna, forged in fire, and she’s ready.


My father, Grayson, clears his throat, the sound booming in the quiet room. His eyes, sharp and piercing, sweep across the assembled elders, then settle on me. “Bolton. Explain what transpired at the clearing.”


I take a deep breath, marshaling my thoughts. This isn’t just a report; it’s a testimony. “Last night, I received a message through the mate bond from Maya. She was in distress. My wolf sensed a full shift, a fight.”


A ripple goes through the elders. Full shifts are rare for newly shifted wolves, and the implications of Maya’s control are not lost on them.


“I arrived at the clearing with Dax,” I continue, my voice steady. “Maya was already engaged with five rogues. She had one of them pinned, severely wounded.” I glance at Kade, who flinches. “The others were retreating, unnerved by her ferocity.”


Grayson nods slowly, his expression unreadable. “And the rogues?”


“They were a scouting party, trained and aggressive. They breached our western border. They knew about Maya. They were there to test our defenses, to capture her.”


“Capture?” one of the elders, a stern-faced woman named Elara, questions, her voice sharp. “Not kill?”


“No. Capture,” Maya interjects, her voice clear and strong. “They were trying to herd me. To take me alive.”


My father's gaze shifts to her, a flicker of something new in his eyes, not just suspicion, but a dawning respect.


“And how did they know to come to that specific clearing, at that specific time?” Elara presses, her eyes narrowing.


This is it. The moment of truth. My gaze locks onto Cassie, who sits a few seats down from Maya, her face carefully blank. She’s trying to project innocence, but I can see the subtle tension in her jaw, the way her fingers clench beneath the table.


“Because they were led there,” I state, my voice cutting through the silence like a knife. “By one of our own.”


The room erupts in gasps and murmurs. Elders lean forward, their faces a mixture of disbelief and outrage.


Grayson’s eyes, cold and hard, fix on me. “Bolton. Be precise. Who do you accuse?”


I don’t hesitate. My gaze never leaves Cassie’s. “Cassie. She sent Maya a text message, luring her to the clearing under the guise of an apology. She was there, watching, when the rogues ambushed Maya. I saw her with my own eyes, melting back into the shadows when I arrived.”


The room explodes. Accusations fly, whispers of “treason” and “betrayal” fill the air. Cassie, finally losing her composure, stands up, her chair scraping loudly across the floor.


“This is outrageous!” she cries, her voice trembling with feigned indignation. “Bolton, you’re letting your… your feelings for Maya blind you! This is a baseless accusation! I was just taking a walk. I happened upon the scene. I was terrified!”


“Terrified?” Maya’s voice is quiet, but it carries an authority that silences the room. “Terrified enough to watch five rogues corner me? Terrified enough to disappear when Bolton arrived, leaving me to face them alone?” Her eyes, usually so gentle, are now dark with righteous anger. “You wanted me gone, Cassie. You wanted my place. You used the rogues to try and achieve it.”


Cassie recoils, her face paling. The carefully constructed mask of innocence cracks, revealing the fear beneath. “I did no such thing! I would never betray my pack!”


“Your ambition blinded you to your loyalty,” I counter, stepping forward. “You fed them information. You knew their movements. You wanted Maya out of the picture so you could step into her role. You thought she was weak. You thought she’d be easy prey.”


Grayson raises a hand, and the room falls silent once more, though the tension remains thick. He looks from me to Maya, then to Cassie, his expression unreadable. This is the crisis. Will he believe us? Will he back his son, his future Luna, against a wolf from a prominent family? The weight of pack tradition, of avoiding internal strife, hangs heavy in the air.


My mother, Rene, who has been quietly observing from her seat beside Grayson, meets my gaze. There’s a silent message there: trust your Alpha. Trust your father.


Grayson’s eyes finally settle on Cassie. “Cassie, did you send a text message to Maya, requesting her presence at the clearing?”


Cassie hesitates, her gaze darting around the room, searching for an ally. There are none. Every eye is on her. “I… I wanted to apologize. I thought it was a good time.”


“Did you know rogues were in our territory?” Grayson’s voice is low, dangerous.


She swallows hard. “No! Of course not!”


“But you were there,” Grayson continues, his voice devoid of emotion, “and you saw Maya under attack, yet you offered no assistance. You fled.”


“I was scared!” she cries, desperation finally breaking through. “I’m not a fighter! I thought… I thought Bolton would handle it!”


“Bolton would handle it,” my father repeats, his voice laced with contempt. “So you used our future Luna as bait. You endangered her, and by extension, the entire pack, for your own selfish gain.”


He stands, his imposing figure casting a long shadow over the table. The authority radiating from him is palpable, silencing even the rustle of clothes. “This pack values loyalty above all else. Betrayal from within is a poison. It weakens us, it corrupts us.” His gaze, cold and hard as granite, fixes on Cassie. “You collaborated with our enemies. You orchestrated an ambush against your future Luna. You are a traitor.”


The words echo through the hall, a pronouncement of judgment. Cassie collapses back into her chair, her face utterly defeated.


“Kade,” Dad barks, turning his attention to the captured rogue. “Tell me. Who contacted you? Who gave you the details of this attack?”


Kade, who has been listening intently, flinches, then lifts his head. His eyes, though still wary, hold a flicker of calculation. He’s looking for an angle, a way to save himself. He knows Cassie is exposed. “It was her,” he rasps, pointing a trembling finger at Cassie. “She told us everything. Where to find the girl, how to get past the patrols. She said the Alpha’s son would be too busy with his new mate to notice.”


A fresh wave of outrage sweeps through the room. Cassie whimpers, burying her face in her hands. There’s no denying it now. The proof is undeniable.


Grayson turns to his Beta, Logan, his voice firm. “Logan, assemble a patrol. Escort Cassie to the edge of our territory. She is to be banished. If she ever sets foot on Stone Mountain land again, she will be treated as a rogue.”


A collective sigh of relief goes through the room. Justice. Swift and decisive.


Logan nods, his expression grim, and two warriors move to escort a sobbing Cassie from the hall. As she passes, her eyes, red and swollen, meet Maya’s. There’s no hatred now, just a raw, desperate fear.


My father turns his attention back to Kade. “And you. You chose to invade our territory. You chose to harm our future Luna. You will be held responsible.” He pauses, his gaze unwavering. “But you spoke the truth. For that, you will be spared the ultimate punishment. You will be held until your Alpha takes responsibility for your actions. If they do not, you will serve your time in our cells.”


Kade nods, a flicker of surprise in his eyes. He expected a harsher fate.


Dad then looks at the assembled elders, his voice regaining its usual commanding tone. “This incident has revealed a weakness in our patrols. It has revealed a dangerous ambition within our ranks. But it has also revealed strength. The strength of our future Luna.” His gaze settles on Maya, and a rare, proud smile touches his lips. “Maya, you showed courage, ferocity, and control. You protected yourself, and you protected this pack. You are a worthy Luna.”


He then looks at me, and his smile widens. “And Bolton. You showed leadership. You trusted your instincts. You stood by your mate.”


A wave of warmth washes over me. This is it. The affirmation I’ve always sought, not just for myself, but for Maya. My chest swells with pride. The tension in the room dissipates, replaced by a sense of unity, of purpose. This pack, my pack, is strong. And with Maya by my side, we are unstoppable.

Later that evening, the pack house is filled with a different kind of energy. The lingering tension has been replaced by a quiet celebration. Even Lila, who usually approaches serious events with a playful cynicism, looks at Maya with wide-eyed admiration.

“You okay?” I ask, pulling her close, my arm around her waist.


She leans into me, her head resting on my shoulder. “More than okay. I think… I think this is what belonging feels like.”


“It is,” I affirm, pressing a kiss to her hair. “And you earned it, every bit of it.”

Then, a small voice pipes up from behind us. “Bolton? Can I talk to you?”

“What is it, Lil?” I ask, a slight frown creasing my brow.


She bites her lip, then gestures subtly toward the door. “That rogue. Kade. He looks… familiar.”


My blood runs cold. Familiar? “What do you mean?”


Lila shrugs, a nervous energy about her. “I don’t know. Just… his eyes. And the way he carries himself. I think I’ve seen him before. With someone.” She pauses, her eyes widening as a new thought strikes her. “A few months ago. With Cassie.” She shakes her head, dismissing the thought. “No, that can’t be right. Cassie wouldn’t…” She trails off, her voice uncertain.


“Cassie wouldn’t what, Lila?” Maya asks gently, stepping forward, her eyes sharp with renewed suspicion.


Lila looks between us, then back at the door, a sudden unease settling over her features. “Cassie wouldn’t… she wouldn’t have been talking to a rogue for months, would she?” Her voice is barely a whisper, but the implication hangs heavy in the air, a chilling new possibility.

And if Kade was involved in that longer game, what else does he know? What other threats are lurking, unseen, just beneath the surface of our newly found peace?


The quiet celebration fades, replaced by a familiar knot of apprehension in my gut. The fight isn’t over. It’s only just begun. And this time, it feels like the shadows are deeper, and the game far more dangerous.

Epilogue
Lila

The pack house finally quiets. Maya and Bolton are probably tucked away, all mate-bonds and powerful. I sneak out, pulling on my favorite worn hoodie, the one with the slightly frayed cuffs. The moon is a fat silver coin hanging in the sky, spilling light through the trees. It’s cold, biting at my exposed skin, but the air feels clean after all the drama.


I jog along the familiar trails, the scent of pine and damp brush a comfort. It’s weird, you know? After everything with Cassie, with the rogues, with Maya finally owning her Luna-ness, it feels… almost normal again. Almost. But I can't shake this restless energy. Everyone’s found their place, settled into their roles. Bolton’s the future Alpha, Maya’s his fierce Luna. Even Mom and Dad seem to have re-found their rhythm. Me? I’m still just Lila. Bolton’s little sister, Maya’s best friend. I’m happy for them, really. But there’s a part of me that longs for that same kind of connection, that undeniable pull. I want my own mate bond to snap into place, that lightning-strike certainty. I want that kind of love, that kind of… everything.


I slow my pace as I near the outer edge of our territory, the place where the trees get thicker and the shadows stretch longer. My wolf, usually a playful, curious thing, suddenly stiffens. A scent, faint but distinct, prickles my nose. Rogue. But it’s not aggressive, not strong enough to be a threat. Just… there. Like something passed through, maybe lingered. I scan the tree line, my heart picking up a beat. Probably just a stray, I tell myself, a deer or something.


Then I see him.


He’s crouched near the old supply cache, a dark silhouette against the moonlit undergrowth. He’s thin, his clothes ripped, rummaging through what little we leave out for lost travelers. My breath hitches. It’s Kade. The rogue Bolton brought back. The one who testified against Cassie. The one who’s supposed to be locked up. What is he doing out here? My hand instinctively reaches for the stone I carry in my pocket, ready to shout, to call for help.


And then, it happens.


It’s like a jolt of pure electricity, a shockwave that slams into my chest and spreads through every cell of my body. My wolf howls, not in warning, not in fear, but in pure, unadulterated recognition. It’s a feeling I’ve read about in stories, heard whispers of from older wolves, but never truly believed until now. The mate bond. It’s snapping, loud and undeniable, a cosmic click that echoes through my very soul.


He looks up, his head snapping toward me. Our eyes lock across the moonlit clearing. His are a startling shade of blue, wide with surprise, then something else. Recognition. His body, tense and wary a second ago, visibly relaxes, a tremor running through him.


For a moment, everything freezes. The air crackles, thick with unspoken meaning, with a connection so profound it steals my breath. My wolf surges forward, a powerful, instinctual pull demanding I go to him, touch him, claim him. But my human brain, the one that remembers everything I just heard in the council meeting, the one that knows who and what he is, screams.


My body moves before I can think, a frantic, desperate flight. I bolt, turning on my heel and running back toward Stone Mountain, toward the safety of my pack, my heart hammering against my ribs, a terrified, bewildered drumbeat. The mate bond, so recently snapped into place, now feels like a burning chain, dragging me back, even as I run. My mind spins, a whirlwind of impossible truths.


My mate is a rogue.

Chapter 1
Maya

The smell of crayons and apple juice fills Rene's living room as I kneel beside six-year-old Emma, helping her color a butterfly that looks more like a purple blob with wings. My hands shake slightly as I guide the crayon, and I hope she doesn't notice.


"You're doing great," I tell her, forcing my voice to stay steady.


Rene asked me to help with the pack's children today while she handles Luna duties. It should be simple. Watch kids. Keep them happy. Don't mess up.


But nothing feels simple when you're the girl who showed up three months ago with no pack, no history, and a mother who won't explain why we left our old life behind.


"Maya, will you read to us?" Seven-year-old Marcus waves a worn picture book in the air.


"Please?" Emma adds, abandoning her crayon to grab my arm.


Soon I'm surrounded by eight kids ranging from four to ten years old, all staring at me with expectant faces. I settle cross-legged on the carpet and take the book Marcus offers.


Little Red Riding Hood.


My stomach tightens. Of course.


"Once upon a time," I begin, opening to the first page, "there was a little girl who lived with her mother at the edge of the woods."


The kids lean in closer. Emma curls against my side, and for a moment, the warmth of her small body eases the knot in my chest.


I flip through pages of Red's journey, the grandmother's house, the huntsman's rescue. When I close the book, ten-year-old Jacob raises his hand like we're in school.


"Is it true?" he asks.


"Is what true?"


"The story. About the big bad wolf eating people."


The other children giggle, but there's something underneath their laughter that makes my skin prickle. Eight-year-old Sophie whispers something to the girl next to her, and they both look at me with curious eyes.


"My mom says Maya's like the girl in the story," Sophie announces. "She was lost in the woods until we found her."


More giggles. Not mean, exactly, but not entirely innocent either.


Jacob nods seriously. "Dad says she doesn't have a pack scent. That's why she got lost."


My cheeks burn. I know what they mean. Even the children can sense I don't fully belong here. The mate bond with Bolton helps, but it's still new, still forming. To their sensitive noses, I probably smell like an outsider trying to fit in.


"Are wolves really monsters?" Emma asks, tilting her head up to look at me.


The room goes quiet. Eight pairs of eyes wait for my answer.


This is it. The moment where I can laugh along, play the part of the lost girl who doesn't know better. Stay safe in my role as the outsider who's grateful just to be here.


Or I can choose something else.


I think about Bolton's steady presence, the way he looks at me like I'm exactly where I belong. I think about Rene trusting me with the pack's most precious members. I think about the children pressed close around me, waiting to see if I'll claim them or keep pretending I'm just passing through.


I smile, and this time it feels real.


"That's just a story," I say, closing the book and setting it aside. "Wolves aren't monsters."


Jacob frowns. "But the wolf ate Red Riding Hood."


"In the story, maybe. But stories aren't always true." I look around the circle of small faces. "Real wolves protect their families. They're loyal and brave and fierce when they need to be, but they don't hurt innocent people."


"Like us?" Emma asks.


"Exactly like you."


Sophie raises her hand. "So you're not really lost?"


The question hits deeper than she probably means it to. Am I lost? Three months ago, I would have said yes. Even three hours ago, walking into this house with sweaty palms and a racing heart, I felt like I was pretending to be someone I'm not.


But sitting here with these children who smell like pack and family and belonging, something shifts inside my chest.


"No," I say quietly. "I don't think I am."


The kids seem satisfied with this answer. They scatter back to their activities, the moment already forgotten in the way of children everywhere. But I stay on the carpet, Emma still curled against my side, and feel something I haven't felt since we arrived in Stone Mountain.


I feel like I might actually belong here.


When Rene returns an hour later, she finds me helping Marcus build a tower of blocks while Emma braids dandelions into my hair.


"How did everything go?" she asks, but she's smiling like she already knows the answer.


"Good," I say, and mean it. "Really good."


Rene settles into the chair across from me, watching as Emma continues weaving flowers through my hair. "They can be a handful."


"They asked about wolves," I tell her. "Whether they're really monsters like in fairy tales."


"Ah." Her expression grows thoughtful. "And what did you tell them?"


"That wolves protect their families. That they're not the monsters in the stories."


Rene nods slowly. "That's exactly right." She pauses, her green eyes studying my face. "You know, they've been talking about you at home. Emma especially. She told her parents you smell like 'pack and flowers.'"


My cheeks warm. "Is that good?"


"It's very good, Maya." Rene's voice is gentle but certain. "It means they're accepting you. Children sense these things before adults do sometimes."


Emma giggles as she adds another dandelion to my hair. "Maya's pretty like a flower Luna."


"She certainly is," Rene agrees, and something in her tone makes me think she means more than just my appearance.


As I walk home later, dandelion stems still tangled in my hair, I replay the afternoon in my mind. The way the children accepted my answer about wolves. The way Emma trusted me enough to fall asleep against my shoulder during quiet time. The way it felt, for just a few hours, like I was exactly where I was supposed to be.


But doubt creeps in as I near the small house I share with my mother. What do I really know about being part of a pack? About being the mate of a future Alpha? The bond with Bolton is still so new, still forming. I can feel it sometimes, like a warm thread connecting us, but what if it's not enough? What if being fated mates doesn't make up for everything I don't understand about this world? About any of this?


As if he can sense my spiraling thoughts, Bolton's voice whispers through our bond. Maya? You okay?


I pause on the front steps, my hand frozen on the door handle. The mind link still catches me off guard sometimes.


I'm fine, I respond, though we both know it's not entirely true.


You don't feel fine. There's warmth in his mental voice, concern threading through our connection. What happened?


I close my eyes, letting the bond steady me. Just thinking too much. The kids today... they were great. But sometimes I wonder if I'm fooling myself.


About what?


About belonging here. About being enough for this pack. For you.


The silence stretches for a moment, and I worry I've said too much. Then his voice comes back, stronger now. Maya, look at me.


I glance around, confused. You're not here.


Turn around.


I spin, and there he is, walking up the path from the direction of the main pack house. His pale blue eyes find mine immediately, and the bond between us hums with recognition.


"How long have you been nearby?" I ask aloud.


"Long enough to know you needed me," he says simply, closing the distance between us.


I pull a dandelion from my hair and twirl it between my fingers. "Maybe I'm not as lost as I used to be, but I'm not sure I'm found yet either."


Bolton steps closer, his eyes tracking the flower in my hands. "The kids did that?"


"Emma. She said I looked like a flower Luna." I can't keep the uncertainty out of my voice.


"She's not wrong." His fingers brush against mine as he takes the dandelion, studying it. "You know what I think?"


"What?"


"I think you're exactly where you need to be." He tucks the flower behind my ear, his touch gentle. "Stop trying to convince yourself otherwise."


The front door opens before I reach it, and my mother appears with worried eyes and tense shoulders.


"How was your day?" she asks, but she's already scanning me for injuries, for signs of trouble. Her gaze shifts to Bolton behind me, and her expression tightens further.


"Fine, Mom. I helped Rene with the kids."


Something flickers across her face. Fear? Regret? It's gone too quickly for me to be sure.


"That's nice, mija," she says, but her smile doesn't reach her eyes. She looks at Bolton again, like she's calculating how much of a threat he represents just by being here. "Come inside. Dinner's ready."


Bolton clears his throat. "Mrs. Ortiz, I can go if this is a bad time."


"No," I say quickly, surprising myself. "Stay. Please."


My mother's jaw tightens, but she steps back to let us both inside. "Of course. There's enough for three."


As I follow her into the house, Bolton close behind me, I can't shake the feeling that while I spent the afternoon trying to figure out where I belong, my mother spent it remembering all the reasons she thinks I don't.


"Maya," Bolton says quietly, his hand brushing my elbow. "You're overthinking again."


I glance back at him. "How can you tell?"


"Your shoulders do this thing when you're spiraling." He demonstrates, hunching slightly. "Plus, the bond. I can feel it."


"Feel what?"


"The way your thoughts are racing. Like a hamster on a wheel."


Despite everything, I almost smile. "Did you just compare my mind to a hamster?"


"If the wheel fits." His eyes crinkle at the corners. "Talk to me. What's really going on?"


I lower my voice as we follow my mother toward the kitchen. "She's scared, Bolton. Look at her."


"I see it."


"And I don't know how to fix it. How to make her understand that this pack, you, all of it... it's not what she's running from."


Bolton stops walking, catching my hand. "Maybe that's not your job to fix."


"What do you mean?"


"I mean maybe she has to figure out her own fears. You can't carry everyone else's baggage and still find your own path."


His words hit deeper than I expected. "When did you get so wise?"


"Alpha training," he says with a slight smile. "Plus, my dad says the same thing to me about pack members who resist change. You can lead them to water, but you can't make them drink."


"Maya? Bolton?" My mother's voice carries from the kitchen, tight with forced politeness. "Dinner's getting cold."


Bolton squeezes my hand. "Ready?"


"No. But let's go anyway."


"That's my girl," he murmurs, and the warmth in his voice makes me believe, for just a moment, that maybe I really am exactly where I belong.


And now, with Bolton here as a living reminder of the pack world she's tried so hard to keep me from, that fear is written all over her face.

Chapter 2
Bolton

The council chamber feels smaller than usual with twelve pairs of eyes watching my every move. I stand at attention beside my father's chair, hands clasped behind my back, while the pack's senior members debate territory patrol schedules like it's a matter of life and death.


It might be, considering how tense everyone's been since the rogue sightings last month.


"The eastern border needs reinforcement," says Marcus Webb, our head tracker. "Too much open ground for a small patrol to cover effectively."


My father nods. "Bolton, what's your assessment?"


This is a test. Everything's been a test since he announced I'd be taking over Alpha duties within the year. Every question, every decision, every moment I'm in this room.


"Marcus is right about the vulnerability," I say, keeping my voice steady. "But splitting our forces leaves the western approach thin. I'd rather rotate larger patrols on shorter shifts than spread everyone across both borders."


"Sensible," Grayson says, and I feel a small knot of tension ease in my chest.


That's when Cassie speaks up from her seat near the back.


"Speaking of things that need controlling," she says, her voice carrying just enough innocence to make it dangerous, "how are we handling the Maya situation?"


The room goes quiet. I feel every muscle in my body lock into place.


"What situation?" I ask, though I already know where this is heading.


Cassie tilts her head, blonde hair catching the overhead light. "Well, she's been here three months now, and no one really knows anything about her background. Her mother won't discuss their old pack. There are no records, no references." She pauses, letting that sink in. "And now Rene has her watching our children."


"Maya has done nothing to warrant suspicion," I say, my voice coming out harder than intended.


"Maybe not," Cassie agrees with a slight smile. "But that's not really the point, is it?"


Marcus shifts in his seat. "What exactly are you suggesting, Cassie?"


"I'm suggesting that a future Alpha should be able to provide his pack with basic information about his mate. Her lineage, her former pack, why they left." Cassie's eyes find mine across the room. "If you can't even get those answers from her, how exactly do you plan to lead the rest of us?"


Heat rises in my chest, but I force my voice to stay level. "Maya and her mother are under pack protection. That's all anyone needs to know."


"Is it, though?" This comes from David Poston, one of the older council members. "We're not questioning the girl's character, Bolton. But Cassie raises valid concerns about transparency."


My father remains silent, watching. Another test.


"The eastern families are asking questions," adds Sarah Martinez. "They're concerned about formal interactions with our pack when they don't know who the future Luna really is."


"What kind of reassurance?" I ask.


"The normal kind," Cassie says sweetly. "Pack lineage, family history, why a Luna and her daughter would abandon their territory with no explanation."


The temperature in the room seems to drop. Everyone knows Elena was a Luna. Not everyone talks about it.


"Perhaps," David says carefully, "it would be helpful if Maya's mother could address some of these concerns directly."


"Elena Ortiz doesn't owe anyone explanations about her past," I say.


Cassie leans forward. "But you do, Bolton. As future Alpha, you owe this pack transparency about the woman who'll be our Luna."


"Maya will be an excellent Luna."


"Based on what evidence?" Cassie's mask of politeness finally slips. "She has no pack training, no leadership experience, no connections to establish her credibility. She spent an afternoon with some children and suddenly she's qualified to help lead us?"


Marcus clears his throat. "That might be a bit harsh, Cassie."


"Is it? We're talking about the future of this pack." She stands, addressing the room rather than me. "I've been here my entire life. I know every family, every tradition, every challenge we face. I've trained for leadership roles since I was old enough to shift. But because fate supposedly chose an outsider with a mysterious past, I'm expected to step aside and watch her stumble through responsibilities I could handle in my sleep?"


"Fate doesn't make mistakes," I say.


"Doesn't it?" Cassie's eyes glitter. "Because from where I sit, it looks like fate chose someone who makes you weak, not strong. You can't get basic information from her mother. You can't integrate her into pack leadership. You spend more time protecting her feelings than addressing legitimate concerns." She pauses, letting her words hit their target. "If you can't even control your mate, Bolton, how do you expect to control a pack?"


The words land like a physical blow. Control. Like Maya is some kind of problem to be managed instead of the woman I'm falling in love with.


The council chamber falls silent. I can feel my father's attention, heavy and evaluating. I can feel the other members waiting to see how I'll respond. Whether I'll defend Maya or try to appease their concerns.


Whether I'll choose my mate or my pack.


The answer isn't even close.


"Let me be very clear," I say, and my voice carries the weight of every Alpha lesson my father ever taught me. "Maya is my fated mate and your future Luna. She has shown nothing but respect for this pack and care for its members. Any questions about her loyalty or worthiness are questions about my judgment, and I won't tolerate them."


Cassie's eyes widen slightly. She wasn't expecting that level of authority in my response.


"As for controlling her," I continue, taking a step forward, "I don't want to control Maya. I want to support her, protect her, and help her find her place among us. Because that's what mates do. That's what Alphas do. We build strength through unity, not dominance through fear."


The room stays quiet, but the energy has shifted. Several council members nod slightly. Marcus looks impressed.


"Furthermore," I say, my eyes finding Cassie's, "any pack member who has concerns about Maya's integration is welcome to speak with me directly. What they're not welcome to do is spread doubt and suspicion in council meetings or anywhere else."


"Bolton." My father's voice cuts through the tension. "A word?"


He stands and walks toward the chamber's side door. I follow, acutely aware of the whispers starting behind us.


In the small adjoining office, my father closes the door and turns to face me.


"Well," he says after a moment. "That was either very good or very stupid."


"Which do you think?"


He studies my face for a long moment. "I think you just showed more Alpha presence than you have in months of training sessions."


Relief floods through me. "So very good?"


"This time." His expression grows serious. "But Cassie isn't wrong about everything, Bolton. The pack does need transparency. And Elena's secrecy is becoming a problem."


"Maya doesn't know the full story either," I say. "Her mother has kept her in the dark about most of it."


"Then maybe it's time that changed."


I nod, though the thought of pushing Elena for answers makes my stomach tighten. She looks at me like I'm a threat to Maya's safety just by existing.


"In the meantime," my father continues, "you handled Cassie well. But be careful. She's not going to let this go."


"I know."


"Do you?" He moves closer, his voice dropping. "Because that girl has been planning for a Luna position since she was old enough to understand what it meant. She's not going to step aside gracefully."


"What are you saying?"


"I'm saying watch your back. And watch Maya's."


We return to the council chamber, where the meeting continues with discussion of winter preparations and supply inventories. Normal pack business. But I can feel the undercurrent of tension, the way certain members avoid my eyes while others seem to be evaluating me with new interest.


When the meeting finally ends, Cassie approaches me near the door.


"That was quite a speech," she says.


"It wasn't a speech. It was a warning."


Her smile is sharp. "Was it? Because it sounded like a young man who's letting his feelings cloud his judgment."


"My judgment is fine, Cassie."


"Is it?" She steps closer, lowering her voice. "Because while you're busy defending Maya's honor, real threats are circling this pack. The rogue sightings aren't random, Bolton. There's something bigger happening, and you're too distracted by your mate bond to see it clearly."


"What are you talking about?"


"Ask your father about the pattern of attacks. Ask him why rogues would be testing our borders right now, right when the pack's future leadership is... uncertain."


She walks away before I can respond, leaving me standing in the empty council chamber with more questions than answers.


My phone buzzes. A text from Maya: How was the meeting?


I stare at the message, thinking about Cassie's words, about the doubt I saw in some council members' eyes, about the way my father warned me to watch our backs.


Fine, I text back. Can I see you tonight?


Of course. Everything okay?


I start to type "yes," then delete it. Maya deserves honesty, even when the truth is complicated.


We need to talk, I send instead. About the pack. About us.


That sounds ominous.


Not ominous. Just... important.


There's a pause before her response comes through: I'll be ready.


As I walk home through the evening air, I can't shake the feeling that something is shifting in the pack's dynamics. Cassie's challenge wasn't just about Maya's background or my leadership. It was about power, about who gets to decide the pack's future.


And if she's right about the rogue attacks being part of something bigger, then Maya and I are walking into a storm that's about more than just acceptance or belonging.


My phone buzzes again. This time it's a message from my father: Council meeting went better than expected. But stay alert. Change always brings out resistance.


I pocket the phone and pick up my pace. Maya is waiting for me, probably wondering what kind of conversation requires advance warning. The truth is, I'm not entirely sure myself.


But I know one thing with absolute certainty: whatever's coming, we'll face it together. The pack can doubt her background, question her qualifications, or challenge my judgment.


None of that changes the fact that Maya is mine, and I'm hers.


Everything else, we'll figure out as we go.

Chapter 3
Maya

The training grounds smell like sweat and determination, but all I can think about are Cassie's words from yesterday echoing in my head. She doesn't belong here. She's weak. She's going to get someone hurt.


I shake my head, trying to focus on the basic combat drills Dax is demonstrating. Around me, a dozen pack teens move through the exercises with fluid grace, their movements speaking of years of practice I don't have.


"Remember, it's about balance and leverage," Dax calls out, his easy grin making everyone feel at ease. "You don't have to be the strongest if you're the smartest."


I attempt the defensive stance he showed us, but my footing feels awkward. Emma's older brother Jake partners with me, patient and encouraging even though I'm clearly struggling.


"Like this," he says, adjusting my arm position. "And when I come at you, just—"


Cassie's voice cuts across the training ground. "Maya, you're telegraphing every move. Anyone could see that block coming from a mile away."


Heat floods my cheeks. She's not wrong, but the way she says it makes several other teens glance over with curious expressions.


"Thanks for the feedback," I manage.


"Just trying to help." Cassie's smile is sharp. "We all want you to succeed here."


The lie in her voice makes my chest tight. I know she doesn't want me to succeed. She wants me to fail publicly so everyone can see what she sees: that I don't belong.


"Let's try the sequence again," Dax says, clearly trying to diffuse the tension.


I reset my stance, but my hands are shaking now. Jake comes at me with a slow, controlled strike, exactly like we practiced. I should block, pivot, counter. Simple.


Instead, something hot and wild flares in my chest.


The world sharpens around the edges. Jake's movement seems too slow, too predictable. The sound of other teens training becomes overwhelming—every footstep, every breath, every heartbeat pounding in my ears.


I move to block, but my hand doesn't feel like mine anymore.


Claws. Actual claws slide from my fingertips, razor-sharp and gleaming in the afternoon sun.


Jake's eyes widen in alarm as my claws slash through the air where his arm was a split second before. If he hadn't jerked backward on instinct, I would have opened his forearm to the bone.


The training ground goes silent.


Everyone is staring. At my hand, still partially shifted, claws extended like weapons I have no idea how to control. At Jake, who's backing away with fear written across his face. At me, standing frozen in the middle of it all.


"Maya?" Dax's voice sounds far away.


The claws retract as suddenly as they appeared, leaving me staring at my normal human hand like it belongs to someone else. My heart hammers against my ribs, and I can taste copper in my mouth.


What just happened?


"I'm sorry," I whisper, but Jake is still backing away, and everyone is still staring, and Cassie is watching with an expression that looks almost satisfied.


"Are you okay?" I ask Jake, louder now.


He nods quickly, but I can smell his fear. Sharp and sour in the air between us.


"Maybe we should call it a day," Dax suggests gently.


"Yeah." My voice comes out rough. "Yeah, I think that's a good idea."


I turn and walk away from the training grounds, my legs feeling unsteady beneath me. Behind me, I can hear the whispers starting. The speculation. The fear.


By the time I reach the tree line, I'm running.


I don't stop until I'm deep in the woods, where the only sounds are my own ragged breathing and the distant call of birds. I sink down against a large oak tree and bury my face in my hands.


Claws. I had claws.


I look down at my hands, still trembling from what just happened. I've only shifted once before—when Cassie challenged me shortly after I arrived and I lost control completely. Since then, I haven't been able to figure out how to shift when I want to, how to control it. My mother always said it would come with time, that some wolves struggle with control in the beginning. I accepted that explanation because it was easier than wondering if there was something fundamentally broken with me.


But this wasn't shifting. This was something else. Something uncontrolled and dangerous.


I could have hurt Jake.


The thought makes my stomach lurch. Sweet, patient Jake who was just trying to help me learn. Who probably goes home tonight and tells his parents that the Alpha's mate is unstable.


That she's dangerous.


My chest tightens as Bolton's voice whispers through our mind link. Maya? I heard there was an incident at training. You okay?


News travels fast in a pack. Of course he already knows.


I hesitate, not sure how to explain what happened when I don't understand it myself. Can you come find me? I'm in the woods behind the training ground.


His response comes immediately, warm with concern: On my way.


I lean back against the tree and close my eyes, trying to make sense of what happened. The surge of heat in my chest. The way the world suddenly felt too sharp, too bright. The claws that appeared without warning and disappeared just as quickly.


What if it happens again? What if next time, someone actually gets hurt? The image of Jake's startled face flashes through my mind, the way he jerked backward just in time. A second later and those claws would have raked across his skin. Blood. Pain. All because I can't control whatever is happening to me.


My hands shake as I stare at them, searching for any sign of the claws that emerged without warning. Nothing. Just normal fingers, normal nails, no hint of the razor-sharp weapons they became. It's almost worse that there's no evidence, like my own body is lying to me about what it's capable of.


What kind of Luna can't control her own wolf? What kind of mate puts her partner's pack at risk just by existing? The questions spiral through my mind, each one sharper than the last. Maybe Cassie is right. Maybe I am too dangerous, too unstable, too broken to be here.


Footsteps approach through the underbrush, and I know without looking that it's Bolton. The mate bond hums with recognition, and some of the panic in my chest eases.


"Maya?" His voice is gentle.


I open my eyes to find him crouched in front of me, pale blue eyes full of concern.


"I almost hurt Jake," I say without preamble.


"But you didn't."


"I had claws, Bolton. Actual claws. I've only shifted once, and I couldn’t control it then either. Suddenly I'm growing weapons in the middle of combat training?" My voice cracks. "What if I can't ever control it? What if someone gets hurt because I don't know what I'm doing?"


He settles beside me against the tree, close enough that our shoulders touch. "Tell me exactly what happened."


So I do. The embarrassment of struggling with basic moves. Cassie's cutting remarks. The strange surge of heat and wildness. The moment my hand stopped feeling human.


"Everyone was staring," I finish. "And Jake looked scared. Of me."


Bolton is quiet for a moment, processing. "Have you ever felt anything like that before? The surge, I mean."


"No. Never." I turn to look at him. "Is that normal? For wolves to partially shift like that?"


"It can happen when we're learning control. Strong emotions, adrenaline, feeling threatened." His fingers find mine, warm and steady. "The fact that you pulled back before anyone got hurt shows you have more control than you think."


"It doesn't feel that way."


"I know." He squeezes my hand. "But partial shifts during high stress are actually a good sign. It means your wolf is strong, just inexperienced."


"My mother never told me any of this. She never prepared me for what it might feel like or how to handle it."


Something flickers across Bolton's expression. "Your mother has her reasons for keeping things from you."


"What reasons? What aren't you telling me?"


He's quiet for so long I think he might not answer. Then: "I think that's a conversation you need to have with her."


"Bolton."


"I'm serious, Maya. There are things about your family, your background, that I can't explain because they're not my secrets to tell."


Frustration rises in my chest. "So I'm just supposed to figure out how to control mystery wolf abilities with no guidance?"


"No." His voice is firm now. "You're going to let me help train you."


I blink at him. "What?"


"Private training sessions. Away from the group, away from Cassie's commentary, away from anyone who might make you feel self-conscious." He turns to face me fully. "I'll teach you control, help you understand what you're feeling, work with you until partial shifts become intentional instead of accidental."


"You would do that?"


"Maya." His hand comes up to cup my cheek. "I would do anything for you."


The sincerity in his voice makes my breath catch. This close, I can see the gold flecks in his blue eyes, can smell the cedar and pine scent that's uniquely his.


"What if I hurt you during training?"


"You won't."


"How can you be so sure?"


"Because even when you were scared and out of control today, you pulled back. Your instincts are to protect, not harm." His thumb brushes across my cheekbone. "Trust me on this."


Something warm unfurls in my chest. Not the wild heat from earlier, but something deeper. Steadier.


"Okay," I whisper.


"Okay?"


"I'll let you train me."


His smile is soft and relieved. "Good."


We sit in comfortable silence for a moment, his hand still cradling my face. The late afternoon light filters through the leaves above us, casting shifting patterns across his features.


"Bolton?"


"Yeah?"


"Thank you. For not making me feel like a freak about this."


"Never." His voice is rough now. "You're not a freak, Maya. You're powerful. There's a difference."


The way he looks at me when he says it makes my heart skip. Like I'm something precious. Something worth protecting and cherishing.


Like I'm exactly who he wants me to be.


"We should head back," I say reluctantly. "People will be wondering where we are."


"Let them wonder." But he stands and offers me his hand. "Come on. We'll start training tomorrow morning. Early, before anyone else is awake."


I let him pull me to my feet, and for a moment we're standing very close. Close enough that I have to tip my head back to meet his eyes. Close enough to feel the warmth radiating from his body.


"Maya," he says quietly.


"Yeah?"


"What happened today? It's going to be okay. I promise."


I believe him. Standing here in the dappled sunlight with his hand in mine and the mate bond humming between us, I actually believe him.


"Tomorrow morning," I say. "I'll be ready."


"I know you will be."


As we walk back through the woods together, I can't help but think that maybe partial shifting in front of the entire training group wasn't the disaster I thought it was.


Maybe it was just the beginning of figuring out who I'm supposed to be.

Chapter 4
Bolton

The western border patrol should be routine: check the fence line, mark territory, watch for signs of rogue activity. Simple. Mindless. Exactly what I need after yesterday's council meeting and Maya's training incident.


But as I approach the clearing near Sunset Ridge, voices carry through the afternoon air. Teen voices, laughing and joking around the old fire pit where pack kids have been gathering for generations.


I should turn back. Give them their space. But something in the tone of their laughter makes me pause behind a cluster of pine trees.

Use the mind link, I send back, then immediately reach out through our mate bond. Maya? Can you hear me?


There's a pause, then her voice whispers through our connection, tentative but warm. Is this better?


Much better. The intimacy of her thoughts touching mine eases some of the tension in my chest. We need to work on strengthening this connection. Texts can be intercepted, overheard. The bond is just ours.


Okay. I can feel her curiosity through the link, along with something else. Worry. You sound tense. What happened on patrol?


I’m Fine, I send through our bond. See you tonight?


Always. Seriously, everything okay?


The mate link. Of course she can sense my agitation.


Just pack politics, I respond. Nothing I can't handle.


But as I pocket the phone and continue my patrol, I can't shake the feeling that Cassie is right about one thing. She did learn something important today.


She learned exactly how to get under my skin.


And in a pack where perception often matters more than reality, that knowledge makes her dangerous in ways I'm only beginning to understand.


My wolf paces restlessly beneath my skin, still agitated from the confrontation. The urge to find Maya, to hold her and reassure myself she's safe, is almost overwhelming.


But I force myself to finish the patrol route. To check every marker, every fence post, every potential weak point along our territory line.


Because Cassie might be wrong about Maya's strength, but she's not wrong about the responsibilities I'm inheriting. The pack does need a leader who can balance emotion with strategy, who can protect what matters without losing sight of the bigger picture.


I just have to figure out how to be that leader while staying true to the mate bond that's become the center of my world.


As the sun starts to set behind the mountains, I head home with one thought echoing in my mind:


Maya isn't my weakness.


She's my strength.


And I'll prove that to anyone who dares suggest otherwise.

Chapter 7
Maya

Athin veil of mist clings to the ground, swirling around my ankles as I walk. Today is different. Today, I’m not just showing up; I’m fighting. The image of Bolton’s unwavering gaze, his utter belief in me, is a shield against the creeping doubt. I clench my fists, digging my nails into my palms. I can do this.


Bolton is already there, of course, a dark silhouette against the pale eastern sky. He’s leaning against the gnarled trunk of an ancient oak, arms crossed, watching the sun paint the horizon. He looks… solid. Unmovable. And for a fleeting second, that sense of unwavering strength makes me feel small, like a wisp of mist that might evaporate in his presence. But then I remember his words: “You’re the bravest person I know.” And the small, fierce core of me flares brighter.


He straightens as I approach, a half-smile playing on his lips. “Morning, Luna.”


The title, spoken so casually, still makes my breath catch. Luna. It’s a heavy cloak, ill-fitting and unfamiliar, but I refuse to let it weigh me down. Not today. “Morning, Alpha.” I try to match his easy tone, but my voice feels a little too tight, a little too eager.


“Ready?” he asks, his pale blue eyes searching mine.


I nod, a decisive jerk of my head. “More than ready.” My gaze sweeps the clearing. It’s empty, just like yesterday. Just us. No judging eyes, no whispered taunts. Just the quiet understanding between two people connected by something far older and deeper than simple words.


He leads me to the center of the clearing, the same spot where I’d almost shredded his arm, where I’d cried over my lack of control. But the memory doesn’t bring the same crushing shame today. It brings a spark of defiance. Never again.


“Same as yesterday,” he says, his voice a low, steady rumble. “Close your eyes. Find her.”


I obey, letting my eyelids flutter shut. The world instantly shrinks, becoming a symphony of subtle sounds: the rustle of leaves, the distant call of a bird, Bolton’s steady breaths beside me. I push past the surface thoughts, the anxiety, the lingering echoes of Cassie’s cruel words. I search for that familiar presence within, the one I’d finally acknowledged, finally understood.


She’s there. A quiet strength, a watchful intelligence. My wolf. I don’t fight her this time; I don’t try to cage her. I invite her. Come.


The heat starts in my chest, a gentle warmth at first, like a banked fire slowly catching. Not a sudden, violent flare, but a gradual, controlled rise. It spreads through my limbs, tingling at my fingertips and toes. It’s a familiar sensation, but this time, there’s no panic. There’s a strange sense of… anticipation.


“I feel her,” I whisper, a tremor of excitement in my voice. “The heat is coming, but it’s… controlled.”


“Good. Breathe with it. Don’t let it overwhelm you.” Bolton’s voice is closer now, a warm current against my ear. “Guide it, Maya. You’re not fighting her; you’re directing her.”


I focus on his words, on the subtle push and pull of the energy within. My fingers begin to tingle more intensely, and I can almost feel the tips of my nails elongating. Not full claws, not yet, but a definite shift. A partial shift, just on the cusp of visible transformation.


Hold it. I command my wolf, a silent plea that feels like a shared breath. Just a little longer.


The tingling intensifies, a delicious stretch of sensation. I can feel the power surging, wanting to burst forth, but I hold it back, reining it in with a newfound focus. It’s like holding a wild bird gently in my hands, not crushing it, but not letting it fly away either. The image of Jake’s arm flashes, then fades. I push it away. This isn’t that.


“I’m… I’m holding it,” I breathe, a shaky laugh escaping my lips. My eyes are still closed, but I can feel the energy just beneath my skin.


“You are,” Bolton murmurs, his voice laced with pride. “Keep breathing. Stay focused.”


I can feel the edges of my nails, sharp, pressing against my skin. They’re longer, harder. I’m doing it. The excitement sparks through me, threatening to destabilize my focus. I rein it back in. Just a little longer.


A full minute stretches, feeling like an eternity. The energy is intense, demanding, but I cling to the control, to the image of Bolton’s face, to the promise of what this means. I am not prey. I am not weak.


Then, slowly, deliberately, I begin to retract. I imagine the energy flowing back, like a tide receding. The tingling subsides, the pressure lessens, and my nails return to normal.


I open my eyes, gasping slightly, a triumphant laugh bubbling up. “I did it!”


Bolton is beaming, a genuine, unrestrained smile that lights up his entire face. It’s a rare sight, and it makes my own heart swell. “You did, Maya! That was excellent. Almost perfectly controlled.” He steps closer, closing the small distance between us. “You held it longer than yesterday, and the retraction was smooth.”


My chest feels light. “I felt it! I felt like I was actually… guiding it, not just fighting it.”


“That’s the key,” he says, his eyes shining. “Working with her, not against her.”


A wave of pure exhilaration washes over me. I’ve done it. I’ve taken a step. A real, solid step forward. The hope that had been a tiny, flickering ember now flares into a warm, steady flame.


“Again?” I ask, my voice eager.


He hesitates, a faint shadow crossing his face. “Are you sure? It takes a lot out of you.”


I shake my head. “I’m sure. I want to keep going. I feel like I’m almost there.” The energy is still buzzing inside me, not demanding, but ready. I want to push. I want to prove to myself, to him, to anyone who doubts, that I can master this.


He studies me, his expression softening. “Okay. But take it easy. Don’t push too hard.”


I close my eyes again, the process a little easier this time. I find my wolf faster, embrace the rising heat with more confidence. The tingling starts, the nails lengthen, and I hold it. I focus on the sensation, on the delicate balance of power. Control.


This time, I feel a subtle shift, a deeper connection. It’s like a muscle I’m learning to flex, awkward at first, but gaining strength with each attempt. I feel the full length of my nails, sharper, more pronounced than before, but still contained. Still mine.


I hold it for what feels like an even longer time, pushing the boundaries, testing my limits. The energy is vibrant, powerful, humming through my entire being. I can do this. I can really do this.


Then, a flicker. A tiny tremor. The control wavers, a hairline crack in my concentration. The heat intensifies, threatening to surge. I’m pushing too hard, too fast.


“Maya?” Bolton’s voice is a lifeline, pulling me back from the brink of losing control.


I quickly retract, feeling the energy flow back, but it’s not as smooth this time. A slight shudder runs through me as my nails snap back to normal.


I open my eyes, a tiny frown creasing my brow. “It was harder that time. I almost lost it.”


Bolton is watching me, his expression a mix of concern and understanding. “You pushed yourself. That’s okay. It’s how you learn your limits.” He steps closer, his hand reaching out, then hesitating. “You did incredibly well, though. You felt the edge, and you pulled back.”


“But I wanted to go further,” I confess, a pang of frustration echoing through me.


“And you will.” He finally reaches out, his fingers brushing against my arm, a warm, comforting touch. “But not all at once. Small victories, Maya. Each one builds on the last.”


His touch sends a wave of warmth through me, banishing the last bit of frustration. It’s not just physical; it’s an affirmation, a shared moment of triumph and understanding. The mate bond sparks between us, a silent language of connection.


He smiles again, a softer, more intimate smile this time, and my breath hitches. “You’re incredible,” he says, his voice low and sincere. “To come back here after yesterday, to face that fear… that’s strength, Maya.”


My cheeks warm under his gaze. “I couldn’t have done it without you,” I whisper, meaning every word. His presence, his belief, is the anchor that keeps me from drifting into panic.


“You did the work,” he counters, his thumb gently stroking my arm. “I just reminded you of what you already had inside.”


We stand there for a moment, bathed in the growing light of the morning, his hand a warm weight on my arm. The scent of pine and damp earth surrounds us, no longer laced with unease, but with the fresh promise of a new day. This tiny clearing, which had felt like a battleground yesterday, now feels like a sanctuary.


“So,” I say, a shy smile spreading across my face, “tomorrow morning, same time?”


He nods, his blue eyes crinkling at the corners. “Same time. We’ll get you there, Luna.” His voice is a quiet promise.


As we walk back toward the pack house, the sun now fully risen, painting the sky in fiery hues, my heart feels lighter than it has in days. The training win, small though it is, feels monumental. It’s not just about controlling my wolf; it’s about controlling my fear, about embracing a part of myself I’d always run from. And it’s about the silent strength of the hand still clasped in mine, the steady presence beside me. Believing in me.

Chapter 8
Bolton

Ifind Maya by the lake, tucked into the roots of the ancient willow that dips its branches into the water. She’s writing in a worn notebook, her brow furrowed in concentration. The morning light catches the auburn strands of her hair, turning them to fire. She doesn’t notice me at first, lost in her own world, and I take a moment to simply watch her. The peace here is a stark contrast to the coiled tension I’ve carried since leaving the elders’ meeting.


Her shoulders are slumped, a shadow of weariness around her eyes even after yesterday’s breakthrough. The small victory at training, the moments of connection, they don’t erase the weight of everything else. Cassie’s words still echoing. The rogue tracks are a fresh wound.


I take a step, and a twig snaps under my boot. Her head snaps up, eyes wide, then softening when she sees me. A small, genuine smile touches her lips.


“Bolton,” she says, her voice a soft murmur that somehow cuts through the unease. “Hey.”


I walk over, careful not to disturb the quiet. The air around the lake is still and cool, carrying the scent of damp earth and growing things. I sit beside her, not too close, but close enough that our knees almost brush. The mate bond pulses, a low, steady current beneath my skin.


“You’re out here early,” I observe, my voice quiet.


She sighs, a feather-light sound. “Couldn’t sleep. Too much buzzing in my head.” She gestures vaguely at her temple. “Good buzzing, mostly. But still buzzing.”


I nod, understanding without needing more words.


She closes her notebook, placing it carefully on the ground between us. Her gaze drifts to the lake, mirroring the stillness of the water. She pauses, picking at a loose thread on her jeans. “I heard about the elders’ meeting. And Cassie.”


My jaw tightens. Of course, she heard. News travels fast in the pack, especially when it concerns its future Luna. “Don’t listen to her, Maya.”


She turns her head, her brown eyes meeting mine. “It’s hard not to. She has a way of… making you doubt yourself.” A flicker of vulnerability, quickly masked. “She has a point sometimes. I can’t exactly protect myself, let alone the pack, if I can’t even fully shift.”


“She doesn’t have a point,” I insist, my voice rougher than I intend. “She’s trying to get under your skin. To make you believe you’re not worthy. Don’t let her.”


“But what if she’s right?” she whispers, the words barely audible. There it is. The fear I’ve been trying to shield her from, the seed of doubt Cassie so expertly planted.


I turn fully toward her, closing the small gap between us. My hand hovers, then settles on her arm, a firm, reassuring pressure. Her skin is warm beneath my palm. “She’s not right, Maya. You faced your fear yesterday. You pushed through it. You showed more courage in that clearing than Cassie will in her entire life.”


Her eyes search mine, and I pour every ounce of my conviction, my belief in her, into that gaze. “You are going to be a phenomenal Luna. The pack needs your compassion, your strength. And your wolf, the one you’re learning to trust, is going to be fierce.”


A slow breath escapes her, and something in her eyes softens. The tension drains from her shoulders, just a fraction. “You really think so?”


“I know so.” My thumb strokes her arm lightly, a small, unconscious movement. The mate bond sings, a quiet affirmation.


She leans back against the willow trunk, a hesitant smile playing on her lips. “Okay, fine. I’ll believe you.”


We sit in comfortable silence for a moment, the only sounds the rustling of leaves and the gentle lapping of the lake. The sun rises higher, painting the water with streaks of gold. This quiet, this shared space, it’s a balm to the chaos.


“So,” she says, breaking the spell, a mischievous glint in her eyes. “Heard you pulled Alpha rank on the elders yesterday.”


A low chuckle escapes me. “I did what was necessary.”


“‘Youthful zeal,’ was it?” she teases, mimicking Elder Thomas’s gruff voice.


I roll my eyes. “They’ll get over it.”


“You think your dad’s okay with you taking charge like that?”


“He’s… adjusting. He knows I protect what’s ours.” Ours, I think, the word resonating with a deeper meaning than I intended.


She nods slowly. “He seems like a good Alpha. Strong.”


“He is.” I pause, then confess, “But sometimes I think he values tradition more than… flexibility. He’s always been rigid.”


“Sounds like someone else I know,” she says, a playful challenge in her gaze.


I raise an eyebrow. “Oh? And who might that be?”


“The Alpha-in-training who thinks he has to carry the entire pack’s burden by himself instead of leaning on his Beta. Or his mate.” Her eyes sparkle with humor, but there’s a serious undertone.


I pull my hand back from her arm, caught off guard. “I’m not… I just need to prove I can handle it.”


“You don’t have to prove anything to me, Bolton.” Her voice drops, softer now, but firm. “I see your strength. And sometimes, strength means knowing when to ask for help.”


Her words hit me, sharp and true. She’s right. I’ve been so focused on projecting an image of control, of unwavering leadership, that I’ve forgotten the power of connection, of shared burdens.


“I… I appreciate that, Maya.” I look away, suddenly feeling exposed. “It’s not easy, this whole Alpha thing.”


“I’m sure it’s not,” she says gently. “But you don’t have to do it alone.”


The air between us thickens, charged with unspoken emotions. The easy banter has faded, replaced by something heavier, more potent. I feel the pull of the mate bond growing stronger, a magnetic force drawing us together.


I turn back to her, and her eyes, wide and luminous, are already on me. The morning sun has fully risen, casting a warm glow on her face, highlighting the freckles dusting her nose. A stray strand of hair has escaped its tie, falling across her cheek.


Step closer or back away?


My wolf growls, an insistent rumble. Closer.


Her lips are parted slightly, a silent invitation. Her gaze drops to my mouth, then back to my eyes, a question shimmering there. My heart pounds against my ribs, echoing the frantic beat of the mate bond.


Each breath I take is shallow, caught in my chest. The world narrows to just her, just us, the quiet pulse of the bond the only sound.


I reach out, my fingers tracing the line of her jaw, gently pushing that stray strand of hair behind her ear. Her skin is soft, heated under my touch. Her eyes flutter closed for a brief second, then open again, dark and expectant.


“Maya,” I breathe, her name a prayer on my lips.


Her answer is a soft sigh, her head tilting into my touch.


I lean in, slow, giving her every opportunity to pull away. But she doesn’t. Her eyes remain locked with mine, a silent permission, a hungry anticipation.


When our lips finally meet, it’s a jolt of pure electricity. Soft at first, a tentative exploration, then deepening as she responds, her hand coming up to cup my cheek, her fingers tangling in my hair. The mate bond explodes, a supernova of sensation, warmth, and belonging.


Her scent, earth and rain and something uniquely hers, fills my senses, intoxicating me. The kiss is fierce, demanding, everything I’ve been holding back, everything I’ve denied myself. Her lips are soft and yielding, and I taste the sweetness of her, the promise of a future I’ve only dared to dream of.


It’s everything. It’s more than everything. It’s the answer to every doubt, every fear, every challenge. It’s the anchor I didn’t know I was missing.


Then, with a force that feels like tearing myself apart, I pull back.


We’re both breathless, chests heaving. Her cheeks flushed. Her eyes are dark, heavy-lidded, still locked on mine. The air around us crackles with the lingering energy of the kiss, charged and potent.


I rest my forehead against hers, trying to steady my racing heart. The mate bond is a roaring fire, burning brighter than ever before.


“Bolton,” she whispers, her voice raw, a question wrapped in desire.


I pull back just enough to look at her, my thumb still tracing the curve of her cheek. Her lips are still parted, inviting. My wolf howls, demanding more, but I know I can’t. Not yet.


“I need to finish my patrol,” I say, the words feeling clumsy, inadequate. My voice is rough, strained.


A flicker of disappointment crosses her face, quickly replaced by understanding. She nods, a small, almost imperceptible movement. “Okay.”


I stand, pulling her gently up with me. Our hands linger, fingers entwined, for a beat too long, until I force myself to release her.


“I’ll see you later,” I promise, my gaze lingering on her face, on the slight flush on her cheeks, the lingering heat in her eyes.


She nods again, a small, shy smile returning to her lips. “Later, Alpha.”


I turn and walk away, heading toward the western border. The cold bites at my exposed skin, but I barely feel it. My lips still tingle, the taste of her still on my tongue. The mate bond still pulsing, a warm current that anchors me, grounds me.


The rogues, the elders, Cassie’s whispers. They’re all still there, but they feel… distant. Muted. Because now, I have this. This undeniable, breathtaking connection. This promise.


Maya isn't my weakness. She’s my strength. And that kiss, that electrifying, soul-shaking kiss, is all the proof I’ll ever need.


I’ll make sure the pack understands it too.

Chapter 9
Maya

The training clearing looks different at night. Moonlight filters through the canopy, painting everything in silver and shadow. My breath forms small clouds in the cold air as I wait for Bolton, nerves buzzing under my skin like live wires.


We've been training every morning for two weeks now, small victories building on each other. But tonight feels different. Tonight, I asked him to meet me here after dinner, because I'm tired of incremental progress. I'm tired of being careful.


I'm tired of being afraid.


The sound of footsteps on frost-brittle leaves announces his arrival. Bolton emerges from the tree line, his dark hair catching moonlight, concern etched across his features.


"Maya? What's wrong?" He's at my side in three quick strides, his hand finding my arm automatically. The touch sends warmth spiraling through me, chasing away the cold.


"Nothing's wrong," I say, though my voice wavers slightly. "I just... I need to try something. Tonight. Here."


His pale blue eyes search mine in the dim light. "What kind of something?"


"I want to push further. Past the partial shift." The words tumble out in a rush before I can second-guess myself. "I know we've been taking it slow, building up gradually, but I feel like I'm ready for more. Like I'm holding myself back."


Bolton's jaw tightens. "Maya, we've talked about this. Too much too fast—"


"Could lead to losing control, I know." I step closer, close enough to see the gold flecks in his eyes, close enough to smell cedar and pine and something uniquely him. "But what if it doesn't? What if I'm stronger than we think?"


The mate bond connecting us, electric and insistent. I can feel his wolf responding to mine, can sense the way his pulse quickens when I'm near.


"You are strong," he says quietly. "But strength isn't about taking unnecessary risks."


"Isn't it, though?" I challenge. "Every morning, I play it safe. I hold back. I do exactly what's expected and nothing more. But that's not who I want to be, Bolton. That's not the Luna this pack needs."


Something shifts in his expression. The concern is still there, but underneath it, I catch a glimpse of pride. Of understanding.


"What exactly are you asking me to do?" he asks.


"Trust me." I take his hands in mine, feeling the calluses on his palms, the strength in his fingers. "Just... trust me to know my own limits."


He's quiet for a long moment, studying my face in the moonlight. Then, slowly, he nods.


"Okay. But the second I tell you to stop—"


"I'll stop," I promise.


We move to the center of the clearing, to the same spot where I've practiced control, where I've learned to find my wolf without fear. But tonight, the energy feels different. Bigger. More demanding.


I close my eyes and reach inward, past the familiar warmth, past the gentle presence I've grown comfortable with. I dive deeper, searching for the full power I've only glimpsed in moments of crisis.


She's there. My wolf. But she's not the calm, watchful creature from our morning sessions. She's wild, magnificent, barely contained. She's been waiting for this moment, for me to finally acknowledge what we could be together.


Heat explodes through my chest.


Not the gentle warmth I'm used to, but a raging fire that spreads through my limbs like lightning. My fingers burn, my spine arches, every nerve ending comes alive with supernatural energy.


"Maya." Bolton's voice cuts through the sensation, steady and grounding. "Breathe through it."


But I don't want to breathe through it. I want to embrace it.


The heat intensifies, and I feel my bones beginning to shift, muscles stretching and reforming. Not just my nails this time—my entire hands are changing, fingers elongating, palms broadening. The transformation is exhilarating and terrifying and completely beyond anything I've experienced before.


"That's enough," Bolton says, his voice sharper now. "Pull back."


I hear him, but I can't seem to obey. The wolf is too close to the surface, too eager for freedom. My back bows as my spine begins to lengthen, and a sound escapes my throat that's half-gasp, half-growl.


"Maya!" Bolton's command cracks like a whip. "Look at me!"


My eyes snap open, meeting his across the clearing. He's moved closer, close enough to touch me if he wanted to, but his hands remain at his sides. His face is a mask of controlled concern, but his eyes… his eyes hold absolute faith.


"You're in control," he says, his voice low and certain. "This is your wolf, your power. You decide how far this goes."


His words anchor me, pulling me back from the edge of complete transformation. I can feel the wolf pushing, demanding release, but I push back. Not yet. Not all the way.


But I don't retreat to the safety of partial shift either.


Instead, I hold myself suspended between human and wolf, letting the power flow through me without overwhelming me. My hands remain partially shifted, claws extended, muscles coiled with supernatural strength. Silver and brown fur begins to emerge along my arms, a beautiful pattern that catches the moonlight like precious metal. The transformation ripples across my skin in waves—patches of rich brown interwoven with streaks of silver that seem to glow in the darkness. But my mind stays clear, focused on Bolton's steady gaze.


"How long can you hold it?" he asks.


"I don't know," I growl, the words emerging rougher than intended, my voice caught somewhere between human and wolf. There's a resonant quality to it now, deeper, carrying an undertone that vibrates in my chest like distant thunder.


The energy is intoxicating, like standing in the eye of a hurricane. "It feels... incredible."


And it does. For the first time since discovering what I am, the wolf doesn't feel like a separate entity I'm trying to control. She feels like part of me. Like the missing piece I never knew I'd lost.


Minutes pass. Or maybe hours. Time seems meaningless when you're balanced on the knife's edge between two forms. The moon travels across the sky above us, and still I hold the partial shift, marveling at the strength flowing through my partially transformed body.


"Maya," Bolton says eventually, and there's something new in his voice. Wonder. "You're doing it. You're actually doing it."


The pride in his tone sends warmth flooding through me, different from the supernatural heat of the shift. This is purely human emotion, joy, triumph, the intoxicating rush of finally proving myself.


I did it. I'm doing it. Right now, in this moment, I'm everything I've dreamed of becoming.


The realization hits me like a physical blow, and suddenly the careful control I've maintained shatters. Not into chaos, but into pure, crystalline certainty.


I am strong. I am worthy. I am Luna.


The energy snaps back into me all at once, the shift reversing so quickly it leaves me gasping. My hands return to normal, my spine straightens, every supernatural sensation fades, but the emotional high remains.


I'm laughing and crying at the same time, adrenaline and relief and pure joy coursing through my veins like champagne.


"I did it," I gasp, looking at my perfectly normal hands in amazement. "Bolton, I actually did it."


"You were magnificent," he says, and the raw admiration in his voice makes my heart skip. "Maya, that was... I've never seen anything like it."


He's standing close now, close enough that I can see the way the moonlight catches in his dark hair, can count the rapid rise and fall of his chest. The mate bond thrums between us, alive with shared triumph and something deeper.


Something that's been building for weeks.


"I couldn't have done it without you," I whisper, taking a step closer. "You believed in me when I couldn't believe in myself."


"You had it in you all along," he says, his voice rough. "I just reminded you to look."


We're barely a breath apart now, the space between us charged with electricity. His eyes drop to my lips, then back to my eyes, a question I can read as clearly as words.


But I'm done with questions. Done with hesitation and fear and playing it safe.


I rise up on my toes and kiss him.


It's nothing like our careful, tentative kiss by the lake. This is fierce and demanding and full of the wild energy still singing in my veins. I pour everything into it. My gratitude, my triumph, my growing feelings for this boy who's never wavered in his faith in me.


For a heartbeat, he goes still with surprise. Then his arms come around me, pulling me against him as he kisses me back with matching intensity. His lips are warm and sure, his hands tangling in my hair, and the mate bond explodes between us like fireworks.


This is what I've been missing. This connection, this certainty, this feeling of coming home.


When we finally break apart, both breathing hard, his forehead rests against mine.


"Maya," he whispers, my name a prayer on his lips.


"I know," I breathe back, understanding exactly what he means.


Everything has changed. Not just my control over my wolf, not just my confidence in my abilities. This. Us. We’ve shifted into something deeper, more real, more permanent.


His thumb traces my cheek, gentle despite the calluses on his hands. "Are you sure? About this, about us?"


I think about everything that's led to this moment. The fear, the doubt, the gradual building of trust and attraction. The way he's stood by me through every stumble and breakthrough. The way he looks at me like I'm something precious and powerful at the same time.


"I've never been more sure of anything in my life," I tell him.


His smile is radiant, transforming his entire face. "Good. Because I'm not letting you go, Luna."


The title doesn't make me flinch anymore. Instead, it settles around my shoulders like a cloak that finally fits.


"I'm not going anywhere, Alpha," I promise.


As we walk back toward the pack house hand in hand, moonlight silvering the path ahead of us, I feel the last of my doubts fall away. I am strong enough for this life, for this pack, for this incredible boy who saw my potential before I did.


I am exactly who I'm supposed to be.


And for the first time since arriving at Stone Mountain, I'm not afraid of what comes next.

Chapter 10
Bolton

The western border is quiet.


I stand at the edge of our territory, breathing in the crisp morning air, and for the first time in weeks, I don't smell the acrid stench of rogue presence. No fresh tracks mar the frost-covered ground. No broken branches or disturbed undergrowth signal unwanted visitors.


Just peace.


Dax emerges from the tree line, shaking his head before I can even ask. "Nothing on the northern perimeter either. Whatever scared them off, it worked."


I nod, feeling tension I didn't realize I was carrying drain from my shoulders. Three weeks of heightened patrols, of watching every shadow, of sleeping with one ear open. But it's paid off. The rogues have moved on, found easier prey elsewhere.


"Good," I say, pulling out my phone to update my father. "Maintain regular patrol schedules, but we can scale back the overnight watches."


"Copy that." Dax grins, his relief obvious. "Think I can actually get a full night's sleep now?"


"Don't get too comfortable," I warn, but I'm smiling too. The constant vigilance was wearing on all of us. It'll be good for pack morale to ease up, to let people breathe again.


As we head back toward the pack house, Dax chattering about his plans for the evening, I find my thoughts drifting to Maya. To last night in the clearing, to the way she'd pushed past every limit I'd tried to set, to the kiss that still makes my pulse race hours later.


She's incredible. Fierce and beautiful and stronger than she knows. The memory of her partial shift, silver and brown fur rippling across her skin like liquid moonlight, sends heat spiraling through my chest.


My Luna. The thought settles in my mind with quiet certainty.


We crest the hill overlooking the pack house, and I spot her immediately. She's in the front yard with a cluster of the youngest pack members, children ranging from five to ten years old. They're playing some elaborate game that involves a lot of running and shrieking with laughter.


Maya's in the center of it all, her dark hair catching sunlight, her face bright with genuine joy. She's chasing a little girl with pigtails, making exaggerated monster noises that send the child into fits of delighted giggles.


"Maya's gonna catch you, Rosie!" one of the boys calls out.


"Never!" Rosie shrieks, darting behind a tree.


Maya prowls around the trunk, her movements deliberately clumsy, giving the girl time to escape. When Rosie bolts for the safety of the porch, Maya throws her hands up in mock defeat.


"Too fast for me," she declares, collapsing dramatically onto the grass. "I'm defeated!"


The children swarm her immediately, piling on top of her in a tangle of small arms and legs. Her laughter rings out clear and warm, and something in my chest expands like sunrise.


She looks at home. Completely, utterly at home.


"She's good with them," Dax observes, following my gaze.


"Yeah." My voice comes out rougher than intended. "She is."


It's more than just being good with children, though. It's the way she fits into the scene, natural and easy, like she's always belonged here. Like this has always been her life, her pack, her family.


Hope blooms in my chest, bright and overwhelming. Maybe we're through the worst of it. The rogues are gone, Maya's control is stronger every day, and the pack is starting to see what I've known all along. She's perfect for this life.


That she's perfect for me.


One of the boys, Tommy, notices me watching and waves enthusiastically. "Bolton! Come play with us!"


Maya's head turns at the sound of my name, and when her eyes meet mine, her smile shifts into something softer, more intimate. The mate bond pulses between us, warm and certain.


"I don't know," I call back, walking closer. "Maya looks pretty tough. Not sure I can take her."


"She's not tough," little Rosie announces with the brutal honesty of childhood. "She let me win!"


"Did she now?" I raise an eyebrow at Maya, who's sitting up now, grass in her hair and dirt on her jeans. She looks absolutely radiant.


"I would never," she says, trying for innocent but failing completely. "I'm a very competitive person."


The children dissolve into giggles, clearly not buying it for a second.


"Maya's the best," Tommy declares with fierce loyalty. "She's gonna be the best Luna ever."


Out of the mouths of babes. The simple acceptance in his voice, the way all the children have gravitated toward her without question or doubt, makes my heart swell with pride.


"I think you're right," I tell him, my gaze never leaving Maya's face.


Her cheeks flush pink, but she doesn't look away. The connection between us feels solid, unbreakable, like something we can build a future on.


For the first time since she arrived at Stone Mountain, I let myself really imagine it. Maya as Luna, leading beside me, beloved by the pack. The mating ceremony that would bind us officially in the eyes of the pack and the moon goddess, her hand in mine as we speak the ancient vows that have united Alphas and Lunas for generations. The way she'd look in ceremonial white, silver threading through the fabric like moonlight, her eyes bright with the same certainty I feel burning in my chest. The pack gathered around us, no more whispers or doubts, just celebration and acceptance. And after, when the formal words are spoken and the bonds are sealed, children of our own someday, with her stubborn streak and my protective instincts. A life built on trust and partnership and love.


It's a beautiful vision. Beautiful and finally within reach.


"Bolton."


Cassie's voice cuts through my thoughts like a blade. I turn to find her approaching from the direction of the pack house, her expression unreadable. The children's laughter dims as they sense the shift in atmosphere.


"Cassie," I acknowledge coolly.


Her gaze flicks to Maya, who's brushing grass from her hair, then back to me. "Heard the patrols found nothing this morning. How... fortunate."


There's something in her tone that sets my teeth on edge. Not relief, as there should be. Something sharper. Almost like disappointment.


"The pack's safety is always fortunate," I reply carefully.


"Of course." She smiles, but it doesn't reach her eyes. "Though I can't help wondering if the rogues were ever really as much of a threat as we thought. Sometimes danger has a way of... resolving itself when certain elements are removed from the equation."


Maya stands up, dusting off her jeans, her expression wary. The children cluster closer to her instinctively, sensing tension they don't understand.


"What elements?" I ask, though I'm not sure I want to hear the answer.


Cassie's smile widens. "Oh, you know. Unstable influences. Things that might attract unwanted attention." Her gaze lands on Maya again, deliberate and pointed. "It's amazing how much more peaceful things become when the source of chaos is... properly managed."


Ice forms in my veins. "What are you saying, Cassie?"


"Nothing at all." She shrugs, all innocence. "Just making an observation about cause and effect. About how problems sometimes solve themselves when the right people take action."


The right people. The phrase echoes in my head, setting off alarm bells I can't quite name. There's something here, something important, but it's dancing just out of reach.


"Maya," I say quietly, not taking my eyes off Cassie. "Why don't you take the kids inside? I think Mrs. Patterson has cookies in the kitchen."


Maya hesitates for a moment, clearly picking up on the undercurrents, then nods. "Come on, guys. Who wants snacks?"


The children cheer and follow her toward the house, chattering excitedly. But I catch Maya's glance back at me, concern written clearly on her face.


Once they're gone, I step closer to Cassie. "Say what you really mean."


"I mean exactly what I said." Her voice is light, casual, but there's steel underneath. "The rogue problem disappeared right around the time certain people started taking a more... active role in pack security. It's almost like someone with inside knowledge helped guide them away from our territory."


Understanding hits me like a physical blow. "You."


"Me what?" But her eyes are glittering with malicious satisfaction.


"You've been in contact with them. The rogues." The pieces fall into place with sickening clarity. "You've been feeding them information."


"That's a very serious accusation, Bolton." Her voice carries a warning now. "One you'd need proof to make stick."


And there's the trap. Because she's right—I don't have proof. Just suspicion and a growing certainty that I've been played. That while I've been focused on external threats, the real danger has been right here inside our territory.


"Why?" The word comes out as a growl. "Why would you betray your own pack?"


"Betray?" She laughs, the sound sharp and brittle. "I'm trying to save it. From a weak Luna who can't control her wolf. From an Alpha who's more concerned with his mate's feelings than his pack's survival."


"Maya isn't weak."


"Isn't she?" Cassie steps closer, her voice dropping to a whisper. "She barely managed a partial shift last night. I was watching, Bolton. Hidden in the trees, seeing how long it would take her to lose control and hurt someone. Hurt you."


The admission hits me like a slap. She was there. Watching. Waiting for Maya to fail.


"But she didn't lose control," I say through gritted teeth. "She was magnificent."


"She was lucky." Cassie's mask slips completely now, revealing the bitter jealousy beneath. "And luck runs out. When it does, when she finally snaps and someone gets hurt, the pack will remember who tried to protect them. Who tried to warn them."


"And you think that someone is you."


"I know it is." She straightens, composing herself again. "The rogues are gone for now, Bolton. But they'll be back. And when they are, when Maya's weakness draws them like vultures to carrion, remember this conversation."


She turns to go, then pauses. "Oh, and Bolton? Next time you want to have a private training session with your mate, you might want to check the wind direction. Sound carries farther than you think."


She walks away, leaving me standing alone in the yard, fury and fear warring in my chest.


She was there last night. Watching. Listening. And if she was feeding information to the rogues before, what's to stop her from doing it again?


I pull out my phone, fingers flying across the screen as I text Dax. Need to talk. Now. Emergency.


The response comes back immediately. On my way.


I pace the yard while I wait, my mind racing. How long has Cassie been in contact with the rogues? What has she told them about our patrols, our defenses, our vulnerabilities? About Maya?


The thought of rogues having inside information about Maya's training, about her struggles with control, makes my wolf snarl with protective rage. If they come back… no, when they come back, they'll know exactly how to target her.


Dax appears at a run, his face grim. "What's wrong?"


"Cassie," I say simply. "She's been working with the rogues."


His eyes widen. "What? Are you sure?"


"Sure enough." I fill him in on the conversation, watching his expression grow darker with every word.


"That treacherous—" He cuts himself off, running a hand through his hair. "What do we do? Tell your father?"


"With what proof? It's her word against mine, and she was careful not to admit anything outright." I resume pacing, frustration building. "But I know it was her. I can feel it."


"So what's the plan?"


I stop pacing, meeting his gaze. "We watch her. Every move, every conversation, every time she leaves pack territory. And when she makes contact with the rogues again… because she will… we'll be ready."


"You think they'll come back?"


"I know they will." The certainty settles in my bones like winter cold. "This was never about territory or resources. This was about Maya. About removing her before she can fully claim her place as Luna."


Dax nods grimly. "I'll put together a surveillance rotation. Volunteers only, people we trust completely."


"Good." I look toward the pack house, where Maya is probably still playing with the children, unaware of the danger circling closer. "And Dax? Not a word about this to Maya. Not yet."


"You don't think she should know?"


"I think she's finally found her footing. Finally believes in herself." The memory of her triumph last night, of her radiant smile this morning, strengthens my resolve. "I won't let Cassie's poison touch that. Not until I have to."


As Dax heads off to organize the surveillance, I remain in the yard, watching the pack house. Through the windows, I can see Maya's silhouette moving among the children, hear the faint sound of their laughter.


She looks so happy. So at peace.


I'll do whatever it takes to protect that peace. To protect her. Even if it means going to war with someone I once considered pack.


The rogues will come back. Cassie will make her move. And when they do, I'll be ready.


My Luna deserves nothing less than my absolute protection.


And she'll have it, no matter the cost.

Chapter 11
Maya

I'm halfway to the training clearing when I hear them.


"...little Red's getting better, though. Yesterday, I heard she managed to hold a partial shift for almost ten minutes."


"Red?" The second voice carries a hint of laughter. "That's what we're calling her now?"


"Come on, it fits. Dark hair, red hoodie, wandering into the big bad wolves' territory. Classic fairy tale setup."


My steps falter on the frost-covered path. Red. Like Red Riding Hood. The comparison hits me like a physical blow, and suddenly I'm not Maya-who-held-a-partial-shift-for-ten-minutes. I'm Red-who-stumbled-into-a-world-that-doesn't-want-her.


The voices fade as the two wolves continue their patrol, but the damage is done. The nickname loops in my head, each repetition stripping away another layer of the confidence I've built over the past few weeks.


Red Riding Hood. The helpless girl who needed saving. The outsider who brought danger to everyone around her.


By the time I reach the clearing, my hands are shaking.


Bolton is already there, leaning against our oak tree, but he straightens the moment he sees me. His pale blue eyes narrow with concern.


"Maya? What's wrong?"


"Nothing." The lie tastes bitter on my tongue. "Just... tired, I guess."


He doesn't believe me—I can see it in the way his jaw tightens, the way he steps closer. The mate bond pulls between us, carrying my distress whether I want it to or not.


"You don't look tired. You look upset."


I force a smile, the expression feeling brittle on my face. "I'm fine. Really. Let's just... let's just get started, okay?"


Bolton studies me for another long moment, then nods slowly. "Okay. Same as always. Find your center, find your wolf."


I close my eyes and reach inward, searching for the calm presence that's become familiar over weeks of practice. But instead of the gentle warmth I'm used to, I find chaos. The nickname has stirred up every insecurity, every doubt, every moment of feeling like I don't belong.


Red. Little Red. The girl who brings danger.


The heat starts in my chest, but it's not the controlled burn I've learned to manage. It's wild, erratic, fed by panic and self-doubt. My wolf responds to the emotional turmoil, rising fast and hard, demanding release.


"Easy," Bolton's voice cuts through the noise in my head. "Breathe through it, Maya."


But I can't breathe through it. The heat is building too quickly, spreading through my limbs like wildfire. My fingers begin to elongate, claws emerging sharp and deadly. Not the gradual, controlled shift I've practiced, but something violent and unpredictable.


What if they're right? What if I am just a danger to everyone around me?


The thought sends another surge of power through my system. My spine arches, bones beginning to shift and realign. The transformation is happening too fast, beyond my ability to guide or contain.


"Maya." Bolton's voice is sharper now, carrying the authority of an Alpha. "Look at me. Focus on my voice."


I try to obey, try to find his face through the haze of panic and power, but the world is tilting, changing. My vision sharpens and blurs simultaneously as my eyes begin to shift. The scents around me intensify—pine, earth, Bolton's cedar and snow scent, and underneath it all, the metallic tang of my own fear.


"I can't—" The words come out as a growl, my vocal cords caught between human and wolf. "Bolton, I can't stop it."


He moves closer, his hands raised in a calming gesture, but I stumble backward. The memory of Jake's torn arm flashes through my mind, followed immediately by an image of those same claws raking across Bolton's face.


Red Riding Hood. The girl who brings the wolves.


"Don't come closer," I gasp, my voice distorted by the partial shift. "I could hurt you."


"You won't hurt me," he says with absolute certainty, still moving forward. "Maya, you're stronger than this. You've done this before."


But have I? Or have I just been fooling myself, pretending I have control when really I've just been lucky? The doubt feeds the chaos, and another wave of transformation ripples through my body. Fur begins to emerge along my arms, the familiar silver and brown pattern, but it's patchy and uneven, my wolf caught between forms.


I'm losing myself. The careful boundaries between Maya and wolf are dissolving, and I can feel my human consciousness slipping away beneath the tide of supernatural power.


"Maya!" Bolton's command cracks like a whip, but for the first time since we started training, it doesn't anchor me. If anything, it makes the panic worse, because now I'm not just failing—I'm failing him.


The boy who believed in me when no one else did. The future Alpha who risked his reputation to train me, who defended me against the elders, who looked at me like I was something precious and powerful instead of broken and dangerous.


What happens when he realizes they were right about me all along?


The thought is the final crack in my control. Power explodes outward from my core, wild and uncontained. I feel my bones grinding as they try to reshape themselves, my muscles stretching and contracting in painful spasms. This isn't a controlled shift—it's my body trying to tear itself apart.


A sound escapes my throat, half-scream and half-howl, raw with pain and terror. Through the chaos, I see Bolton's face, his eyes wide with alarm, and the sight of his fear cuts deeper than any physical pain.


I'm scaring him. I'm dangerous. Just like they said.


The realization gives me the strength to do what I should have done from the beginning. Instead of trying to control the shift, instead of fighting for balance between forms, I slam every mental barrier I have back into place. I reject the wolf completely, shoving her down so hard and fast that the backlash sends me to my knees.


The transformation reverses violently, bones snapping back into human configuration, claws retracting, fur disappearing as if it was never there. But the sudden shift leaves me gasping, my body confused and aching, my wolf howling in pain and betrayal from whatever deep place I've locked her in.


"Maya." Bolton is beside me in an instant, his hands hovering over my shoulders but not quite touching. "Are you hurt? Talk to me."


I can't look at him. Can't bear to see the disappointment in his eyes, the realization that maybe Cassie was right after all. Maybe I am too unstable, too dangerous to be Luna. Too weak to be his mate.


"I'm fine," I lie, struggling to my feet on shaking legs. Every muscle in my body feels like I've been hit by a truck, the aftermath of forcing such a violent reversal. "Just... just lost focus for a second."


"Maya—"


"I said I'm fine." The words come out sharper than I intend, but I can't help it. The shame is eating me alive, and his concern only makes it worse. "It happens sometimes. No big deal."


But it is a big deal, and we both know it. Three weeks of progress, of careful building and small victories, wiped out in a matter of minutes. I'm back where I started. Worse than where I started, because now I know exactly how badly I can fail.


Bolton reaches for me, but I step back, wrapping my arms around myself like armor. The mate bond stretches between us, carrying his confusion and worry, but I can't deal with that right now. Can't deal with being the source of his stress, his burden to bear.


"Maybe we should call it a day," I say, still not meeting his eyes. "I'm obviously not in the right headspace for this."


"We don't have to stop. We can talk through what happened, figure out what triggered—"


"No." The word comes out flat, final. "I need some time to think. To... to figure some things out."


I turn toward the path before he can respond, before he can see the tears threatening to spill over. But his voice stops me.


"Maya, wait. Please."


The pain in his voice nearly breaks my resolve. Nearly sends me running back to him, to the safety of his arms and the warmth of his belief in me. But I can't. Not when I'm this much of a mess. Not when I could hurt him.


"I'll see you later, Bolton," I say without turning around. "I just... I need some space right now."


I walk away before he can argue, each step feeling like I'm tearing something inside my chest. The mate bond protests the distance, sending waves of longing and regret through my system, but I ignore it. I have to.


The path back to the pack house blurs through my tears. By the time I reach the edge of the clearing, I'm running, desperate to put distance between myself and the site of my latest failure. Between myself and the look of alarm on Bolton's face when he realized I was completely out of control.


Red. Little Red Riding Hood.


The nickname follows me as I flee, a mocking reminder of everything I'm not. I'm not the strong Luna this pack needs. I'm not the mate Bolton deserves. I'm just a girl who stumbled into a world where she doesn't belong, bringing chaos and danger in her wake.


By the time I reach my room, the tears are falling freely. I collapse onto my bed, burying my face in my pillow to muffle the sobs. My body still aches from the violent shift and reversal, but the physical pain is nothing compared to the emotional devastation.


I've been kidding myself. All those small victories, all that careful progress. It was just an illusion. When it really mattered, when the pressure was on, I fell apart completely. Just like I always do.


The mate bond sizzles, carrying Bolton's concern across the distance between us, but I block it out as best I can. He's better off without my chaos bleeding into his emotions. The pack is better off without a Luna who can't control her own wolf.


Maybe it's time to stop pretending I can be something I'm not. Maybe it's time to accept that some people just aren't meant for this life, no matter how much they want it.


The thought should bring relief, but all I feel is the hollow ache of loss. Loss of a dream I'd barely dared to hope for. Loss of a future I'd started to believe was possible.


Loss of the boy who looked at me like I was worth fighting for.


I curl up on my side, pulling my knees to my chest, and let the tears fall. Outside my window, I can hear the normal sounds of pack life continuing. Children playing, adults talking, the comfortable rhythm of a community at peace.


A community I'm not really part of. Not anymore.


Maybe I never was.
