"I'll help," said Baby Suggs. "You too short to reach the fire." It was dark when Woodruff clicked the horse into a trot. He was a young man with a heavy beard and a burned place on his jaw the beard did not hide. "You born up here?" Baby Suggs asked him. "No, ma'am. Virginia. Been here a couple years." "I see." "You going to a nice house. Big too. A preacher and his family was in there. Eighteen children." "Have mercy. Where they go?" "Took off to Illinois. Bishop Allen gave him a congregation up there. Big." "What churches around here? I ain't set foot in one in ten years." "How come?" "Wasn't none. I dislike the place I was before this last one, but I did get to church every Sunday some kind of way. I bet the Lord done forgot who I am by now." "Go see Reverend Pike, ma'am. He'll reacquaint you." "I won't need him for that. I can make my own acquaintance. What I need him for is to reacquaint me with my children. He can read and write, I reckon?" "Sure." "Good, 'cause I got a lot of digging up to do." But the news they dug up was so pitiful she quit. After two years of messages written by the preacher's hand, two years of washing, sewing, canning, cobbling, gardening, and sitting in churches, all she found out was that the Whitlow place was gone and that you couldn't write to "a man named Dunn" if all you knew was that he went West.
