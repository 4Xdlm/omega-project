For more than three years that window has been lighted, night after night. Those who have been there say that it’s the room inhabited by the wife of Pedro Paramo, a poor, crazy woman who is frightened by darkness. And just look; now the light is turned off. Isn’t that a bad sign?” “Maybe she died. She was very sick. They say that she no longer recognizes people, and she talks to herself. Pedro Paramo certainly got what he deserved when he married that woman.” “Too bad for Don Pedro.” “No, Fausta. He deserves that, and a lot more.” “See, the window is still dark.” “Stop worrying about that window, and let’s go and get some sleep. It is very late for a couple of old women like us to go wandering alone through the streets.” And the two women, who had come out of the church very close to eleven o’clock that night, began to disappear under the arches, watching how the shadow of a man was passing through the plaza in the direction of Media Luna. “Look, Dona Fausta, don’t you think that man walking over there is doctor Valencia?” “It could be, though I’m so weak-sighted I probably wouldn’t recognize him.” “You must remember that he always wears white pants, and a black jacket.
