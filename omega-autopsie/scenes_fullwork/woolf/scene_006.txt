He ought to have been a Duke. He would be certain to marry one of the Royal Princesses. And of course Hugh had the most extraordinary, the most natural, the most sublime respect for the British aristocracy of any human being he had ever come across. Even Clarissa had to own that. She told him that she considered him responsible for the state of ‘those poor girls in Piccadilly’ — Hugh, the perfect gentleman, poor Hugh! — never did a man look more horriɹed! She did it on purpose, she said afterwards (for they used to get together in the vegetable garden and compare notes). ‘He’s read nothing, thought nothing, felt nothing,’ he could hear her saying in that very emphatic voice which carried so much farther than she knew. The stable boys had more life in them than Hugh, she said. He was a perfect specimen of the public school type, she said. No country but England could have produced him.
