Crossly huddled under my blankets, I watched with open hostility as he fidgeted around the room, throwing the windows open, glancing at the maps of Syrtes that littered my desk. “Chilly in here, isn’t it ... What’s this? My, that’s nice, careful work you do ... God, it must have been exciting for you ... ‘the patrol line,’” he murmured significantly, staring at the map with an inquiring look. “I’ll bet you didn’t run any risk of crossing it with Marino. How far from the coast did you get?” he asked with a sly turn of his head. “Marino knows what he has to do, and he can do it without any help from you. Shut that window, Fabrizio—shut it now, or you’ll be sorry. You want me to catch my death? I’m sleeping, do you realize that, sleeping! I think I’d better throw you out of here,” I added, though without much conviction. “‘Throw you out of here’ ... Kindness itself, from my comrade-in-arms ... All right, all right, whatever you say. There’s still time. You can tell me all about it later, as we march along.” “I feel as much like marching as I do like being hanged. Damn it, Fabrizio, close that door!” Fabrizio looked at his watch. “We’ve got about fifteen minutes to get out of here, the two of us, or else we’ll be late. You’d better start getting dressed.” “Once and for all, Fabrizo, go to hell!” Fabrizio shrugged and rolled his eyes with an exaggeratedly pained expression. “All right, Aldo, at least try to pull yourself together.
