I glanced around me, suddenly chilled and isolated by the ashen daylight that hovered in the room with the canal’s reverberation: it seemed as if the tide bearing me on had just withdrawn to its lowest mark and that the room was slowly draining through the black hole of sleep, haunted by bad dreams. With her arrogant immodesty and aristocratic unconcern, Vanessa always left the high doors of her room ajar: in the half- light that fell like a fine ash from the glow of those short days, limbs outspread, heart heavy, I could feel on my skin something like a cold exhalation from that series of high, dilapidated rooms; it was as if the fallen tumult of pillage had forgotten us there, huddled in a corner—as if my ears, listening in spite of myself, were trying to catch in the distance, in the darkness, somewhere in the waiting silence of a beleaguered city, the gusts of a wild hunt. Anxiety would bring me to my feet, and I would find myself standing in the middle of the room—it seemed to me that the distance between objects and myself had imperceptibly increased, as if there had been a movement of faint withdrawal, a sullen and persistent hostility in things. I groped toward a familiar support that suddenly failed, and my equilibrium gave way, like the void that opens before us among friends who already know a piece of bad news.
