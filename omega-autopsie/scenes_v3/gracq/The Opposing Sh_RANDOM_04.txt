Vezzano. This was the limit Marino had assigned for the patrol runs: it was time to rejoin Fabrizio on the bridge. At this hour of earliest morning—as in a city on land—the labyrinth of corridors remained surprisingly empty; a lifeless pallor, which seemed to seep from the metal walls, dimmed the faint halo of the night lamps: I seemed to be floating like a shadow at the center of the gray ship, of the gray day, of the gray sea, my heart subdued in this grim expanse of first dawn. Fabrizio was alone on the bridge. His small head with its boyish face seemed to swim in the huge hood of his sea cloak; his features drawn by a night’s watch made him look even younger. He turned toward me at a creak of the stairs and watched me emerge from the hatchway without a word, his forehead creased in an expression of ill-feigned surprise, and I realized that he had been expecting me. “There’s some hot coffee in the chest under there,” he said without turning around when I had come up to him. “You’d better have some,” he added when I didn’t move. “These early mornings off Syrtes are chilly ... Did you have a good night?” He was staring very meaningfully at the horizon ahead of the ship, his voice eager to fill the silence. He was like a girl who both dreads and longs for a declaration, and I suddenly felt more comfortable. I sipped my coffee, in no hurry now, and stealthily glanced at him from time to time.
