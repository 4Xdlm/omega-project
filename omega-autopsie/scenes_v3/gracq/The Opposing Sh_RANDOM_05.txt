Did Marino ever tell you how he went down with his ship?” She gave me a sidelong glance, and once again there was that tone of intimacy and irony in her voice which she instinctively assumed whenever she mentioned the captain. “It’s scarcely imaginable, Aldo—given his passion for agriculture. But apparently you can’t judge people by the way they look, and maybe it was in a previous life ... When he shows you that hand with the two fingers missing, you can’t help thinking—you know?— of someone who has received the stigmata!” She broke into her silvery laugh. “No one in Syrtes has a service record that compares to Marino’s.” I answered dryly. “Don’t be upset, Aldo ...” Again the delicate, rather fierce laughter mocked me. “You know how much I care for him. We’re old friends. Well, even when they were sinking ... Aldo! I don’t know if you can visualize Marino as a man of the storms, arms folded on the bridge while the ship was going under,” she mused, as though struck in spite of herself by the comic impossibility of the scene. “‘Women and children first’ ...
