The avenues under their vaults of trees seemed abandoned and chilly; I remember thinking that the city was shutting itself up earlier than usual. In the lower town, the mist from the swamps was already filling the streets: the familiar rotting smell passed over my face like the touch of a blind hand and gave me a pang—I had returned. No sooner had the car stopped in front of the house than my father and Orlando appeared behind the grille, while several shutters gaped in the houses nearby. From those piercing looks and the feverishness of my father’s hands as they fumbled with the lock, I realized how apprehensively I had been awaited: in living memory my father had never come in person to open his door to a visitor. “At last you’re here!” he said, seizing my hands with an emotion he could not master as he led me toward the house with great strides. Instinctively, Orlando fell back behind us, intimidated, as if making way for the principal actor; his gaze felt heavy on the nape of my neck, full of embarrassment, respect, and gravity.
