In addition to the several faint and one relatively strong autobiographical reference, there are also several faint allusions to Nabokov’s own fiction, and two strong references to external subtexts, including one to a classic of Russian literature. When the narrator mentions “the ugly, vicious backward children, he was with in that special school,” the description could apply just as well to the school for abnormal children where Adam Krug’s son is killed in Bend Sinister and mention of the young man’s “cousin, now a famous chess player” brings to mind Luzhin in The Defense. Aside from these allusions to his own literature, Nabokov includes an extensive reference to Puskin’s short story “The Queen of Spades.” It occurs in the third section in a short paragraph concerning the old woman: Bending with difficulty, she retrieved some playing cards and a photograph or two that had slipped from the couch to the floor: Knave of hearts, nine of spades, ace of spades, Elsa and her bestial beau. (A13: 163) The pervasive use of the number three in “Signs and Symbols” and the succession of three cards ending in an ace suggest the analogous pattern in Puskin’s story.
