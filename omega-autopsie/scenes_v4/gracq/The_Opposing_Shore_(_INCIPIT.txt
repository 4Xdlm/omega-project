No one in Orsenna knows much more about Farghestan—and no one seeks to know much more—than the fact that the two countries, as we are taught at our school desks, are officially in a state of hostility. It is actually three centuries ago—in a period when navigation had not yet forsaken Syrtes—that continual Farghestani piracies along these desert coasts drove Orsenna to launch an expedition of reprisal, which stood off the enemy coast and bombarded its ports without mercy. Several skirmishes ensued, and then hostilities, which had engaged no major interest on either side, languished and quite died down of their own inanition. Tribal wars paralyzed navigation in Farghestani ports for many years, and Orsenna’s shipping, for its part, slid into a gradual lethargy: one by one, merchant vessels abandoned a secondary sea where commerce was dying out. Thus the Gulf of Syrtes became, by degrees, a veritable dead sea which no one any longer dreamed of crossing; its harbors, silted up, now received only coasting vessels of the smallest tonnage: Orsenna was rumored to maintain, in some ramshackle shipyard, only a few sloops of the least aggressive nature, their sole function being to police the sponge- fishing grounds in the appropriate season.
