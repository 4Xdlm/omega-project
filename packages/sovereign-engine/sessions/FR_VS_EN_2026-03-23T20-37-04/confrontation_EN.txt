The tavern door groaned against Marcus's shoulder like a wounded animal. Smoke and whiskey breath coiled through amber light, wrapping around conversations that died mid-sentence as he stepped inside. His boots found the warped floorboards with deliberate weight.

Twenty faces turned toward him. Nineteen looked away.

The twentieth belonged to Vincent Carelli, perched on a barstool like a vulture claiming carrion. His manicured fingers wrapped around a tumbler of bourbon, the amber liquid trembling with microscopic ripples that betrayed his calm facade.

"Didn't expect company tonight." Vincent's voice carried the practiced smoothness of oil over broken glass.

Marcus moved deeper into the room's belly. "Sarah sends her regards."

The name hung between them like a blade suspended by spider silk. Vincent's jaw tightened imperceptibly, a tell Marcus had memorized during countless poker games before trust became roadkill on the highway of their friendship.

"How is your sister?" Each word dripped with manufactured concern.

"Dead."

The syllable struck the room's atmosphere like lightning splitting oak. Conversations withered. Chairs scraped against wood as patrons created distance, sensing the electrical charge building between the two men.

Vincent set down his glass with surgical precision. "Tragic accident, I heard."

"Accident." Marcus tasted the lie on his tongue, bitter as burnt coffee grounds. "Funny how accidents follow you around like stray dogs, Vincent."

The bartender—a mountain of flesh wrapped in stained canvas—began polishing glasses with unnecessary vigor. His eyes darted toward the baseball bat resting beneath the register, calculating distances and consequences.

"Careful, Marcus." Vincent swiveled on his stool, facing him completely now. "Grief makes men say foolish things."

"Grief makes men see clearly."

Vincent's hand drifted toward his jacket's interior, where Marcus knew a .38 nestled against Italian silk. But Marcus had already closed half the distance between them, his own weapon—Sarah's butterfly knife—singing its steel song as it opened in his palm.

"She trusted you." The words emerged raw, scraped from his throat's depths. "Loved you, even."

"Love's a luxury in our business."

Vincent drew his pistol with practiced fluidity, but Marcus was already airborne, launching himself across the remaining space like a missile guided by vengeance. The knife found flesh before the gun found its target, sliding between ribs with surgical precision.

Vincent's shot went wide, splintering the mirror behind the bar into a constellation of reflecting shards. His bourbon glass shattered against the floor, amber liquid mixing with the darker crimson now pooling beneath his collapsing form.

Marcus knelt beside the dying man, watching light fade from eyes that had once laughed at Sarah's terrible jokes. "She deserved better than your betrayal."

Vincent's lips moved soundlessly, forming words that would never reach the air. His final breath escaped like a deflating balloon, carrying away secrets that would die with him.

The tavern held its collective breath, waiting for what came next.
