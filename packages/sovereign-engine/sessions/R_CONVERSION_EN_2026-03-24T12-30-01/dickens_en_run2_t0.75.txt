Little Timothy Cratchwell—for that was the boy's name, though he might as well have been called Curiosity itself, so thoroughly did that quality pervade his small but determined frame—had been prowling about his grandparents' ancient dwelling with the systematic thoroughness of a customs inspector and the barely suppressed excitement of a treasure hunter, when his exploring fingers discovered what no finger had touched for lo! these many years.

Behind the great mahogany wardrobe—that looming sentinel which had stood guard in the upper corridor since the days when his grandfather's grandfather first drew breath in this very house—lay concealed a door so cunningly hidden that it seemed the very walls had conspired to keep their secret. The wardrobe itself, a monument to craftsmanship and moths in equal measure, groaned like an arthritic giant as Timothy's small shoulders pushed against its bulk, revealing inch by precious inch the outline of what had been lost to memory and dust.

Oh, but what a perfume greeted him then! Not the sweet cologne of his grandmother's parlour, nor the robust aroma of his grandfather's tobacco, but something far more ancient and mysterious—the very breath of time itself, exhaling the accumulated sighs of decades through that narrow crack. It was dust, yes, but dust with a pedigree; dust that had settled upon secrets and slumbered upon forgotten dreams; dust that spoke of old wood seasoned by summers and winters beyond counting, of leather that had known the touch of hands long since stilled, of paper that had yellowed with the patient dignity of age.

The boy's heart hammered against his ribs like a woodpecker possessed of supernatural enthusiasm as he squeezed through the narrow opening, his eyes wide as saucers and twice as bright. What chamber of wonders awaited him? What relics of bygone days lay shrouded in that aromatic darkness? The very air seemed thick with stories untold, with mysteries that beckoned to him across the years like ghostly fingers crooked in invitation.

Behind him, the great house continued its eternal conversation of creaking timbers and settling stones, while ahead stretched the unknown—that delicious, terrifying unknown which calls to every child's soul with a voice sweeter than angels and more compelling than the dinner bell. Timothy Cratchwell, explorer extraordinary, aged nine years and three-quarters, stepped forward into that fragrant darkness, leaving behind the familiar world of nursery rhymes and morning porridge for whatever marvels awaited in that secret chamber where time itself seemed to hold its breath.
