The man sits. The café is empty. It is always empty at this hour. The hour when rain begins.

He checks his watch. The watch his father gave him. The father who never waited for anyone. Never.

The rain hits the windows. Hits and hits. Like fists. Like the fists of children who want to come in. But there are no children here. Only the man. Only the waiting.

He knows.

He knows someone will come. Someone will push the door. The door that never opens at this hour. The door that will open today.

The coffee grows cold. He doesn't drink it. He never drinks the coffee when he waits. Waiting is not for drinking. Waiting is for waiting.

The watch ticks. Ticks like his heart. Like the heart of a man who knows. Who knows without knowing. This is the worst knowledge. The knowledge that comes before knowledge.

Rain. Always rain when life changes. When life breaks. When life begins.

He checks his watch again. The same time. Always the same time when everything changes. When everything breaks. When everything begins.

The café owner wipes glasses. Wipes and wipes. The glasses are clean. They have been clean for hours. But he wipes. He wipes because the man waits. Because waiting makes everyone clean glasses.

The door will open.

The door will open and someone will enter. Someone who carries his life. His new life. His death. Perhaps his death.

He doesn't know who. He doesn't know how. He only knows. Knows like animals know rain. Like children know fear. Like women know men.

The rain stops.

This is when it happens. When rain stops. When silence comes. When the door opens.

He doesn't look at the door. He looks at his hands. The hands that will touch. That will kill. That will love. He doesn't know which. He doesn't know how.

But he knows.

The silence is complete now. Complete like death. Like birth. Like the moment before the door opens.

His watch ticks. His heart ticks. The world ticks toward the moment. The moment when everything changes. When everything breaks. When everything begins.

He waits.

He waits like men have always waited. Like his father waited. Like his father's father waited. For the door. For the person. For the life that comes after life.

The café owner stops wiping. Stops because the moment has come. The moment when doors open. When people enter. When lives change.

The man checks his watch one last time.

Now.

Now the door will open.

Now someone will enter.

Now his life will change.

He doesn't look up. He never looks up when life changes. When life breaks. When life begins.

But he knows.

He knows the footsteps that will cross the floor. The voice that will speak his name. The eyes that will see him. See him as he has never been seen.

The door opens.
