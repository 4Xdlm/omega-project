The child pushes. The wardrobe moves.

Behind it, the door. Small. Low. Made for children perhaps. Or made for secrets.

The handle turns. It turns easily. As if someone turned it yesterday. As if someone turns it still.

The smell comes first. Dust. Old wood. Something else. Something that lived here once. Something that breathed here.

The child enters.

The room is small. Smaller than his bedroom. Smaller than the bathroom. A room forgotten. A room that forgot itself.

There is a window. Dirty glass. Light comes through but changed. Filtered. The light of another time.

A chair. One chair. Facing the window. Waiting.

On the chair, a book. Open. As if someone was reading. As if someone stopped reading to answer the door. To make tea. To die.

The child approaches. The floorboards speak. They remember footsteps. Other footsteps. Heavier ones. Older ones.

The book is about birds. Pictures of birds. Black and white birds on yellow pages. The yellow of time. The yellow of forgetting.

He touches the page. It crumbles slightly. Like skin. Old skin.

There are words written in the margins. A child's handwriting. His father's handwriting perhaps. When his father was small. When his father discovered things.

The words say: "I saw this one. In the garden. It sang."

The child looks up. Through the dirty window. The garden is there. The same garden. But different. Older. The trees are taller. The shadows longer.

He sits in the chair. It fits him. It was waiting for him.

The silence is complete. Not the silence of empty rooms. The silence of rooms that remember. That hold their breath.

On the windowsill, a small box. Inside the box, a feather. Black. White at the tip. Like the birds in the book.

The child holds the feather. It weighs nothing. It weighs everything.

Downstairs, his grandmother calls. Her voice comes from far away. From another world. The world of kitchens and meals and ordinary time.

He doesn't answer. Not yet.

The room holds him. The chair holds him. The silence holds him.

This is his room now. His secret. His inheritance.

He closes the book carefully. Places it exactly where it was. The feather goes back in the box. The box back on the sill.

Everything must stay. Everything must wait.

For his return. For his children. For the endless succession of discoveries.

The child leaves. Closes the door. Pushes the wardrobe back.

But the room remains. Breathing quietly. Holding its treasures.

Holding its time.
