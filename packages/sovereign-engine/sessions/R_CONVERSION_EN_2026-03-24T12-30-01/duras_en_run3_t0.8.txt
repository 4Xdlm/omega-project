The operation is finished.

The patient lives. Lives diminished. Lives less. The hands that cut, that tried to save, they shake now. Imperceptibly. The surgeon sees them shake.

She removes the gloves. The latex peels away like skin. Like the skin she opened. Like the skin she could not repair completely.

The patient will wake. Will wake to a world smaller than before. This is what the surgeon knows. This is what she carries.

The door closes behind her.

The hallway stretches. Empty. White walls that have seen everything. Walls that say nothing. The fluorescent light hums its mechanical song. Always the same song. Day and night, the same.

Her feet move. One step. Another. The rhythm of walking when you have nowhere to go. When going is simply what bodies do. After. After the cutting. After the failing. After the saving that is not saving.

The wall receives her.

She leans. The weight of her body against the white surface. Cool against her back. Through the thin fabric of the surgical gown. Still warm with sweat. With the heat of concentration. Of precision that was not precise enough.

Her eyes close.

Behind the lids, darkness. But not empty darkness. Darkness filled with the memory of what she saw. Inside. The body opened like a book. A book with pages torn. Pages she tried to mend.

The patient's face before sleep. Trusting. The way patients trust. Completely. Without question. They give you their bodies. They give you their lives. They say: make me whole.

But wholeness. What is wholeness.

She breathes. In. Out. The mechanical rhythm. Like the machines that kept the patient alive. That keep them all alive. Breathing for them when they cannot breathe. Beating for them when their hearts forget how to beat.

The hallway remains empty.

This is the hour when the hospital sleeps. When even the dying rest. When the fluorescent light hums alone with its secrets. With all the bodies it has illuminated. All the blood. All the hope. All the diminishment.

Her hands are steady now.

She opens her eyes. The hallway has not changed. Will not change. Tomorrow there will be another operation. Another patient. Another trust given. Another chance to save. To fail. To save imperfectly.

This is what surgeons know. This is what they carry. The weight of flesh in their hands. The weight of lives made smaller. Made different. Made less than they were.

She pushes away from the wall.

Her feet begin to move again. Down the hallway. Toward the next room. The next patient. The next trust. The next diminishment.

The fluorescent light hums.

The hospital breathes.

The surgeon walks.

This is how it continues. This is how it has always continued. Body after body. Cut after cut. Save after save. Fail after fail.

The operation is finished.

The next operation waits.
