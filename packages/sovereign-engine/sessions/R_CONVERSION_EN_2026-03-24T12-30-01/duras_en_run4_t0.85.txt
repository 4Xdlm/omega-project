He comes back. The old man. He comes back to the village.

The stones are the same stones. The walls are the same walls. But something has died. Something has died in the stones, in the walls.

He walks. His feet know the path. His feet remember. But his eyes see nothing they recognize. Nothing.

The baker's house. Still there. The shutters are blue now. They were green. Green like the river in summer. The baker is dead. The baker's wife is dead. The bread smell is gone.

He stops. He listens. The silence is different. It's not the silence of waiting. It's the silence of forgetting.

The church bell rings. Same bell. Same bronze voice calling across the years. But who listens now? Who comes when it calls?

His mother's house. He finds it. The door is red now. It was brown. Brown like her eyes. Like her eyes when she smiled. When she waited for him by the window. The window is broken. Boarded up. No one waits there now. No one.

He touches the door. The wood is cold. Cold like her hands that last winter. That last winter when the snow came and stayed and stayed.

What is he looking for? He doesn't know. Something. Something that lived here once. Something that breathed in these streets, laughed in these doorways. Something that knew his name.

The fountain. Still there. Still singing its water song. The only voice that remembers. The only voice that hasn't changed. He sits on the stone edge. His reflection looks back. An old man. A stranger. Even to himself.

Children used to play here. Their voices. Their voices like birds. Where are the birds now? Where are the children? Gone. All gone.

The grocer's shop. Empty. Windows dusty. Through the glass he sees shelves. Empty shelves. The grocer used to give him candy. Peppermints. Red and white striped peppermints that tasted like Christmas. The grocer called him petit bonhomme. Little man. Who calls him anything now?

Evening comes. The shadows grow long. Longer than they used to be. Or maybe he's smaller now. Smaller than the shadows.

He looks for his childhood. He looks for his mother's voice. For the sound of his father's boots on the cobblestones. For the smell of bread baking. For the laughter of friends long dead.

He finds nothing. Nothing but stones and silence and the water singing alone.

The village is there. But the village is gone. Like everything. Like everyone.

Like him.

He turns away. He walks back to the road. His footsteps echo. Echo and fade. Echo and fade.

The stones remember nothing.
